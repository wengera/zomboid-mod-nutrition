# Recomposition in advanced natural lifters: the evidence on both sides

Harvested on 2026-10-14 by `claude-opus-5-5`, read-only. Every citation was resolved by PMID through NCBI E-utilities (esearch, esummary, efetch), and full texts were read through PMC wherever they are open; where only an abstract was read, the row says so. Rows are in `advanced-recomp-part.tsv` (S3.1-S3.31, provisional; `science_check.py --part`: 0 findings). Studies already in the registers are cited by their ids: Helms 2023 S1.29, Garthe 2013 S1.30, Rozenek 2002 S1.31, Murphy & Koehler 2022 S1.28, Iraki 2019 S1.32, Kouri 1995 S1.1, Ahtiainen 2003 S1.11, Antonio 2015 H.43, Roth 2022 H.47, Barakat 2020 H.48 (unverified), Hulmi 2017 S1911, Hatamoto 2024 S0606, Helms 2014 protein review S0599.

The question has two parts:
- Do natural lifters near their ceiling gain muscle at maintenance or in a deficit?
- Does a surplus beat maintenance for them?

"Advanced" here means several years of training and a fat-free mass index near the natural limit of about 25 kg/m2 (S1.1). Almost no trial recruits that population. The study populations fall into three tiers:
- **Advanced** (>= 5 y, FFMI about 23): Moro 2016, the physique-athlete cohorts (Isola, Mitchell, Buechel), the contest-prep cases, and Ahtiainen's strength athletes (S1.11).
- **Intermediate** (1-5 y, ordinary strength): Helms 2023, Antonio 2015, Hulmi 2017, Haun 2019, Smith 2021, Roth 2023, Campbell 2020.
- **Athletes from other sports** adding a strength programme: Garthe 2011 and 2013.

## 1. Re-check of the earlier evidence

**Antonio 2015 (H.43; S3.21).**
- Population: the high-protein group had 4.9 +/- 4.1 y of training and the normal-protein group 2.4 +/- 1.7 y. The groups differed significantly in training age, which the authors name as a limitation. The SD of 4.1 y means many subjects had only 1-3 y.
- Lean measure: Bod Pod, a two-compartment model whose FFM moves with hydration. The authors cite Utter's finding that Bod Pod, hydrostatic weighing and skinfolds all lose FFM on dehydration.
- Effect against error: FFM +1.5 +/- 2.2 kg (HP) and +1.5 +/- 1.8 kg (NP). That is about 2 % of FFM, inside the 2-3 % that glycogen and water alone move a DXA lean estimate (Bone 2017, S3.22). The individual SD is larger than the mean.
- Energy: "about maintenance" is soft. Intake was self-reported at 28-36 kcal/kg/d (implausibly low for trained lifters), and the HP group ate more than its own baseline yet lost 0.1 kg.
- Verdict: an intermediate group, a wet lean measure, an energy balance that is not controlled. It does not show muscle gain at maintenance in advanced lifters.

**Garthe 2011 (S3.12, S3.13).**
- Population: elite athletes from mixed sports (the 2013 companion lists rowing, kayaking, soccer, volleyball, taekwondo, skating and ice hockey). They added four resistance sessions a week, and their prior lifting history is not in the abstract. They are elite athletes, not advanced lifters, and the strength programme was probably a new stimulus.
- Lean measure: DXA.
- Effect against error: +2.1 +/- 0.4 % lean body mass in the 19 % deficit, against -0.2 +/- 0.7 % in the 30 % deficit. The abstract does not say whether +/- is SD or SE. A 2 % DXA gain is the size of a glycogen and water swing (S3.22), so it is not proven to be muscle. A carbohydrate-restricted deficit would, if anything, lower glycogen and bias DXA lean downward, which makes the slow arm's gain a little more credible.
- Follow-up: by 12 months there was no difference between the arms (S3.13).

**Barakat 2020 (H.48).** The trained-subject table could not be read: journals.lww.com returned HTTP 402, and the row stays open (S3.30). The abstract's claim ("a substantial amount of literature … in resistance-trained individuals") counts "resistance-trained" as anything from about 6 months up. No study I found that it could draw on recruits lifters near the ceiling except Moro 2016, which shows fat loss with FFM held, not muscle gain (§ 2).

**Helms 2023 (S1.29; per-group reading S3.16).**
- Population: lifters with >= 1 y of training and an intermediate baseline (bench 98 kg, squat 1.8 x body mass), only 5-6 per group. The power analysis wanted 31 per group for muscle thickness.
- Muscle at maintenance: maintenance gained 0.4 kg of body mass and +0.25 +/- 0.37 cm of biceps thickness. That is about the same as the 15 % surplus arm's +0.34 +/- 0.54 cm and the 5 % arm's +0.19 +/- 0.10 cm. At the other sites the means clustered near zero in every group.
- Surplus size: the two surplus arms gained the same body mass (3.3 kg), so the 5 %-against-15 % contrast was lost.
- Fat: the skinfold sum went -1.4 (maintenance), +10.0 (5 %) and +12.4 mm (15 %).

**Garthe 2013 (S1.30).** Lean gain did not differ between arms. Ribeiro 2019 reports, second-hand, lower-body FFM +0.5 kg against 0.0 kg in favour of the surplus arm, with fat +1.1 against +0.2 kg. That figure is not in the abstract, so it is unverified and has no row. The population is again elite non-lifters.

## 2. Advanced lifters at maintenance

**Moro 2016 (S3.1)** is the only controlled study that recruits lifters with >= 5 y of continuous training.
- Population: FFM about 73.5 kg at 177-178 cm, an FFMI of about 23.3 kg/m2 (computed here from Table 1). Body fat was about 13 %.
- Conditions: both arms ate 100 % of their estimated needs (about 2.8-3.0 Mcal, 1.9 g/kg protein) for 8 wk under a standardised programme.
- Lean result: DXA FFM moved +0.64 kg (73.08 to 73.72) and +0.48 kg (73.93 to 74.41), both non-significant. Arm and thigh muscle area were also non-significant.
- Fat result: the time-restricted arm lost 1.62 kg of fat (p = 0.0005). Taking that loss at about 9400 kcal/kg and the FFM change at the spec's 1816 kcal/kg, it implies an average deficit of about 250 kcal/d. This is an inference, not a reported value.
- Reading: in advanced lifters at maintenance, or in a deficit of about 250 kcal/d, fat-free mass was held, with a non-significant +0.5-0.6 kg that sits inside the measurement noise. This is recomposition in the "lose fat, keep muscle" sense, and no measurable muscle gain.

**Isola 2023 (S3.2).** The non-dieting controls were former or upcoming physique athletes whose training background mirrored the competitors'. Over about 46 weeks they "showed no systematic changes in any measured variables", DXA and ultrasound muscle size included. Their energy intake was not set (male controls' energy availability rose), so this is an off-season observation, not a maintenance trial. It is consistent with lean gain near zero over most of a year in competitive natural physique athletes.

**Ahtiainen 2003 (S1.11).** Strength athletes training identically to non-strength-trained men for 21 wk:
- strength athletes: MRI quadriceps CSA -1.8 % and force +3.9 %;
- non-strength-trained men: CSA +5.6 % and force +20.9 %.

Energy was not controlled. The study shows that at high training status, gain under a new programme of normal energy is near zero or below. That is the training-status effect itself, separate from energy.

## 3. Contest preparation: lean loss in a deficit

Natural physique athletes in long, moderate deficits (0.3-0.5 kg/wk) on high protein (2.3-3.1 g/kg) and continued training lose fat-free mass in men, and hold or slightly gain it in women.

| Buechel 2026 (S3.4) | 19 natural physique athletes (8 M, 11 F) | weight -7.1 kg, FM -5.8, FFM -1.7 [-2.6, -0.9] over about 21 wk; FFM +2.7 by 6 wk post, +1.6 above baseline at 12 wk | DXA (skipped 2 wk post for glycogen and water) |

Rossow 2013, Pardue 2017 and Schoenfeld 2020 give body-fat percentages or a qualitative "loss of lean mass" in their abstracts only (Rossow 14.8 to 4.5 %; Pardue DXA 13.8 to 5.1 %), so they are open row S3.31.

**Does leanness or training age raise lean loss?** The direction is supported, the size is not measured:
- Schoenfeld 2023 notes the male loss despite slow loss rates and 2.8 g/kg of protein. It speculates that loss accelerates as body fat nears essential levels and points to the universal fall of testosterone to hypogonadal levels in the men.
- Hulmi 2017 cites Dulloo for the proportion of lean loss rising as initial body fat falls. That is the Forbes relation already in the register (S0054, S0055), and the existing partition's Forbes share carries it.
- Roth 2022 (H.47) names training experience, pre-diet volume and deficit size as mediators of lean sparing in trained athletes.

No study isolates training age at fixed leanness. The advanced-lifter penalty in a deficit is, on this evidence, mostly a leanness penalty (Forbes, Alpert's ceiling, the hormonal fall at very low fat). It is not a separate "training age" term.

## 4. Surplus trials in trained lifters

**Ribeiro 2019 (searched for as "Ribeiro 2019 or 2023"; it settles H.66)** is the only trial in competitive bodybuilders. Its muscle outcome is the Lee prediction equation `SMM = 0.244 x BW + 7.8 x H + 6.6 x S - 0.098 x A + R - 3.3` (full text, Methods). That is a linear function of body weight, so the high-energy group's larger weight gain produces its larger "muscle" gain by construction. The trial cannot tell muscle from fat and gives no evidence either way. Two more limits:
- its "natural" criterion was 3 months off steroids;
- its 50 kcal/kg/d "moderate" arm was itself probably a surplus.

**What +15 % gained over maintenance in Helms 2023:**
- biceps +0.09 cm;
- vastus lateralis +0.06 cm;
- triceps +0.03 cm;
- rectus femoris +0.01 cm (-0.21 against -0.22 cm: near-identical declines);
- body mass +2.9 kg, mostly fat by skinfolds.

None is distinguishable from zero at this n. Helms 2023 measured no lean mass by DXA.

**Reading:**
- No trained-subject trial shows that a surplus beats maintenance for muscle with a direct measure. The best one (Helms 2023) is intermediate-level and underpowered, and its regression found a weak link between body-mass gain and biceps thickness only (R2 0.24).
- When a surplus is cut (Gavanda) or doubled (Helms, Garthe 2013), the extra goes to fat.
- Hatamoto (S0606) is the one mechanistic hint that energy itself drives body-protein accretion: none at +10 % from protein, +0.44 kg at +40 %. Its subjects were untrained and did not train.

## 5. Meta-analyses and training-status moderators

- **Murphy & Koehler 2022 (S1.28):** about -500 kcal/d prevents lean gain. As § 10.1 of the spec review already notes, its studies were nearly all in untrained people. No 2023-2026 meta-analysis of deficit against hypertrophy in trained people was found (PubMed searches on energy restriction, resistance training, lean mass, trained, meta-analysis, 2023-2026).
- **"Roth 2023":** the 2022 systematic review (H.47) and the 2023 volume trial in trained men (S3.11). It is not a meta-analysis.
- **Pelland 2026 (H.1):** adjusts for training status but does not report an energy moderator.
- **Smith 2021:** names training status as an expected but unmodelled moderator.

## 6. Measurement caveats

- **Bone 2017 (S3.22):** glycogen loading alone raised DXA lean body mass by 2.0-3.0 %, and depletion lowered leg lean by 1.4 %, with muscle protein essentially unchanged.
- **Nana 2012 and 2013 (S3.23):** exercise, food and fluid before a scan shift DXA lean and raise its typical error by about 10 %.
- **Buechel 2026 (S3.4):** skipped DXA 2 wk post-competition because of a 2-3 % glycogen and water inflation, and called the early FFM rebound "wet mass".
- **Haun 2019 (S3.25):** in trained men (5 +/- 3 y), 6 wk of high volume raised fibre CSA by 23 % while actin and myosin concentration fell about 30 %. Size gains in trained people over short blocks can be sarcoplasmic and fluid expansion rather than contractile protein.
- **Tinsley & Moore 2020 (S3.24):** DXA tracked a 4C model's FFM change well in women who lost fat while gaining FFM. DXA is not biased against detecting recomposition, but its noise floor is about the size of the effect.

**Does the correction erase the trained recomposition findings?** It erases them as proof of muscle gain:
- Antonio's +1.5 kg (about 2 % of FFM, by Bod Pod) and Garthe's +2.1 % (DXA) both sit inside the glycogen and water band.
- Hulmi's DXA +1.1 % was non-significant and disagreed in sign with BIA, skinfolds and ultrasound.

It does not prove them wrong either: a carbohydrate-restricted deficit lowers glycogen, so DXA would under-read muscle there. What survives is "fat-free mass held while fat fell". The direct-measure data in trained people at maintenance (Helms MAIN biceps +0.25 cm by ultrasound; Moro's non-significant anthropometric muscle area) show small changes inside their own noise.

## 7. Expert positions

- **Iraki 2019 (S1.32):**
  - novice and intermediate lifters: +10-20 % and 0.25-0.5 % of body weight a week;
  - advanced bodybuilders: "should be more conservative".

  It argues from rate-of-gain reasoning, not from a maintenance-against-surplus trial in advanced lifters.
- **Helms 2023's own recommendation:** "conservative energy surpluses scaled to RT experience of 5-20 % … such that more advanced trainees consume smaller surpluses" (full text, Conclusion). That is the opposite of "advanced lifters need a surplus to grow". It still assumes some surplus.
- **Helms 2014 (S3.26):** for contest preparation, 0.5-1 %/wk loss to maximise retention and 2.3-3.1 g/kg LBM of protein. **Aragon 2026, the ISSN position (S3.27):** 1.9-3.2 g/kg in lean trained dieters. Both aim at retention in a deficit, not gain.
- **Schoenfeld 2023 (S3.5):** describes the off-season as "customarily" a surplus with the goal of increasing lean mass while minimising fat. It cites practice, not a trial.
- **Buechel 2026 consensus (S3.28):** after a contest, 10-15 % weight regain over 1-6 months. This is a recovery norm, not evidence that a surplus is needed for new muscle.

None of these sources cites a trial showing that an advanced natural lifter grows at a surplus and not at maintenance. The bulk-then-cut position rests on two arguments:
- the rate of gain is very small near the ceiling, so any energy-status penalty matters proportionally more;
- a deficit at very low body fat costs lean mass (§ 3).

The second argument is measured. The first is reasoning.

## 8. Verdict

**In natural lifters near their ceiling, muscle gain at maintenance is negligible in the only controlled data: Moro 2016 found FFM +0.5-0.6 kg (n.s.) over 8 wk at FFMI about 23; the physique controls in Isola 2023 showed no systematic change over about 46 wk; Ahtiainen's strength athletes (S1.11) moved -1.8 % in CSA. In a deficit they hold fat-free mass only while moderately lean, and men lose it at contest leanness (§ 3).**

**Whether a surplus beats maintenance for them is not measured. No trial in advanced naturals compares the two with a direct muscle measure. In intermediates (Helms 2023), and in elite non-lifters (Garthe 2013), a surplus added fat and not detectably more muscle. The one bodybuilder surplus trial (Ribeiro 2019) measures weight, not muscle.**

**So the owner's first half holds and the second is unproven. Near the ceiling, net gain at maintenance trends to about 0. That is driven at least as much by the ceiling itself, which damps gain under any energy, as by energy. "A surplus is needed" is practitioner reasoning with a mechanistic hint (Hatamoto, untrained), not a trial result.**

Confidence: **low to moderate**.
- Moderate that advanced maintenance gain is near zero: one RCT arm pair at FFMI about 23, one cohort, plus the training-status effect in S1.11.
- Low on whether a surplus changes that: no direct trial exists; the population is n <= 17 per study; most lean measures are DXA, BIA or Bod Pod, whose 2-3 % noise is larger than an advanced lifter's 8-week gain.

Evidence quality is mostly small RCTs in intermediates, cohorts and case studies for advanced athletes, and narrative reviews for the advanced-lifter position.

**Model default:** neither A nor B as stated. The evidence supports the in-between:
- recomposition at maintenance or in a moderate deficit is real for novices (Longland S0064) and plausible for intermediates (Garthe 2011, Helms MAIN, Antonio within noise);
- it fades toward zero for advanced lifters;
- the fade comes mainly from the ceiling term the model already has, `k·(F_c − F)`, with a modest status-dependent penalty on the energy gate. It does not come from a hard surplus requirement.

A surplus beyond about +5-10 % should buy fat, not muscle, at every status.

## 9. Recommended status-dependent form

This keeps § 10.2's structure, `g = k·(F_c − F)·G·f_p(p)·f_e(E, s, p)`, and changes only f_e's three anchors. The constants are judgment fitted to bounds, not measured values. Each is listed with what bounds it.

1. **Balance value:** `f_bal(s) = 1 − 0.5·s`, so 1.0 at s = 0, 0.75 at s = 0.5 and 0.5 at s = 1.
   - At s about 0.3-0.5, the balance-against-surplus difference must be undetectable in 8 wk at n = 6 (Helms 2023, S3.16).
   - At s about 0.8-0.9, the total 8-wk gain at balance must stay inside DXA noise, < 1 % FFM (Moro, S3.1). The ceiling term already ensures that, so f_bal(1) is only weakly identified: anywhere from 0.3 to 1 fits.
   - 0.5 honours the owner's prior and Hatamoto's energy-accretion hint (S0606) without contradicting a trial. If the fit wants f_bal(1) = 1, that is Default A and equally consistent with the data.
2. **Saturating surplus:** `surplus_full = +0.10·TDEE` for all s, with no status dependence.
   - Helms 2023: +15 % no better than +5 % or maintenance for muscle, worse for fat.
   - Garthe 2013 (S1.30): guided surplus no better for lean.
   - Gavanda 2026 (S3.20): about 15 % less than the prescribed surplus, same FFM.
   - Iraki (S1.32) and Helms 2023 recommend smaller surpluses for the advanced, not larger.
3. **Deficit zero:** `E₀(s, p) = −500·(1 − 0.5·s)·(1 + a_nov·(1 − s)²·clip((p − 1.6)/0.8, 0, 1))` kcal/d, so -500 at s = 0 and -250 at s = 1 before the protein term.
   - s = 0: Murphy & Koehler, S1.28, a mostly untrained pool.
   - Moderate s: Garthe 2011 (S3.12), where -19 % allowed DXA lean gain and -30 % did not.
   - s about 0.85: Moro's time-restricted arm, an inferred deficit of about 250 kcal/d with FFM held (+0.64 kg n.s., S3.1).
   - Roth 2023 (S3.11): at 30 kcal/kg, roughly -30 %, trained men lost 0.5-0.9 kg of lean mass.
4. **Deficit arm:** keep the Forbes share and Alpert's ceiling as the leanness penalty (§ 3). Add no separate training-age term to lean loss. Contest-prep male lean loss is explained by low fat mass and is not tied to training age at fixed fat.
5. **Surplus arm:** keep "growth first, the rest to fat (and X)". Helms 2023 skinfolds, Gavanda and Garthe 2013 all show the excess going to fat in trained people.

Model checks the fit should pass:
- at s = 0.9, F near F_c and balance for 8 wk, ΔF < 0.5 % with fat steady;
- at s = 0.9 and +10 %, ΔF no more than about 2x the balance value and still < 1 %, with fat gain carrying most of the weight;
- at s = 0.5, the balance and +15 % runs are within one ultrasound noise band of each other over 8 wk;
- at s = 0.9 and -500 kcal/d, ΔF ≤ 0 at a fat mass near contest levels.
