# Fatigue and sleep: evidence base for a realism nutrition model

Scope: sleep need and sleep debt, the two-process model as a quantitative shape, sleep loss on cognitive and physical performance, the nutritional and hydration determinants of fatigue, caffeine and alcohol pharmacology on alertness and sleep, exercise and sleep. Audience: the design of a Project Zomboid mod that owns the fatigue-accrual and sleep-recovery formulas and feeds them from energy availability, nutrient grades, hydration, caffeine and alcohol.

## How to read this

Every row carries a grade for the kind of evidence behind it:

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| MA | meta-analysis or systematic review of human studies |
| RCT | randomised controlled trial or randomised laboratory protocol (the Dinges-group sleep-restriction protocols sit here: they randomise dose in a controlled laboratory environment) |
| COH | cohort, cross-sectional, case series, or a controlled human depletion/dosing experiment that is not randomised |
| AUTH | authoritative report or consensus statement — AASM/SRS, National Sleep Foundation, EFSA, WHO, IOM/NASEM |
| TXT | textbook, narrative review, or expert consensus without a systematic search |
| MODEL | a published mathematical model with fitted parameters — cited for its *shape and constants*, not for an effect size |

Three conventions matter.

**Every citation was verified by fetching its record.** Each DOI or PMID below was confirmed against the Europe PMC REST API (`ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search`) or Crossref, and the title, author list and year were matched. Where only a record was reachable and not the number, the row says **not verified in this run** and the number is not used. Where a number came from an open-access full text rather than an abstract, the row names the full text.

**The session's web-search budget was exhausted before this report began**, so every source here was found and verified through the Europe PMC search API directly. That biases discovery toward papers whose titles contain the search terms, and it means a well-known paper with an oblique title may be missing. `## Gaps` names the specific searches that could not be run.

**The distinction between *supplementing a replete person* and *depriving a person* runs through § 3 exactly as it does in the vitamins report.** Most modern meta-analyses test supplementation of already-replete people and mostly find nothing. A survival game cares about the depletion direction, so the deficiency and depletion literature carries the weight here and the supplement trials mainly mark where a proposed mechanic is *unsupported*.

**A note on the engine's units.** Vanilla fatigue is a `[0,1]` stat with the `TIRED` moodle at 0.6/0.7/0.8/0.9, sleep removing the top 0.7 over a nominal 5 h and the last 0.3 over a nominal 7 h, and awake accrual at `3.45e-5` per tick scaled by endurance deficit, a sleep trait, the thermoregulator and a rest divisor (jar report §§ B–C). Throughout, **`F` means the engine's fatigue stat** and **`S` means the two-process model's homeostatic sleep pressure**; § 7 states the mapping between them, and it is not the identity.

**Interpretation for a game model.** The two-process model hands the design exactly the shape it needs and, unusually, hands it *numbers*: a saturating exponential rise with a wake time constant of 18.2 h and an exponential fall with a sleep time constant of 4.2 h, which means sleep pressure sheds roughly four times faster than it builds. That asymmetry is the single most important fact in this section, because vanilla Project Zomboid already encodes it crudely — 16 h of waking against a nominal 5–7 h of sleeping — and the model says the crude version is right in kind. The twin thresholds (0.67 and 0.17 about a mean, with a circadian amplitude of 0.12) give a defensible mapping onto the engine's `[0,1]` fatigue stat and even onto its `TIRED` moodle ladder, since the model's own upper threshold sits at 0.67 and vanilla's first Tired level fires at 0.6. Sleep debt is real, cumulative and dose-dependent: two weeks at 6 h costs as much as two whole nights without sleep, so a model that lets a player run on short sleep indefinitely without a slowly worsening penalty is contradicting the strongest evidence in the field. Crucially, Van Dongen's subjects did not *feel* proportionally worse — subjective sleepiness lagged the objective deficit badly — which licenses a design where the moodle understates the accumulated debt, and that is a rare case where a game mechanic and the evidence point the same way. Repayment is monotonic in the recovery dose but **not complete in one night**: after five nights at 4 h, even ten hours of recovery sleep left deficits, so the model should carry a slow debt term that a single long sleep only partly clears. On the sleep environment, the honest reading is that the engine's bed ladder (floor 0.60 up to good-bed-plus-pillow 1.15) has the right ordering and the wrong pedigree — the only systematic review found says a medium-firm surface is best and gives no effect size, so the ladder's *magnitudes* stay a game judgement. Heat above 25 °C degrading sleep is the one environmental number worth wiring, and the engine already has a thermoregulator to read it from.

**Interpretation for a game model.** The evidence has an unambiguous ordering and it is not the one a naive game model would guess: **mood is hit hardest, then simple sustained attention, then executive function, and reasoning is barely touched at all.** Positive mood collapses at g = −0.94 while reasoning accuracy sits at a non-significant g = −0.125, so a tired character in Project Zomboid should be miserable and slow to react long before becoming stupid — which argues for driving the mod's unhappiness and stress coefficients from fatigue at least as hard as anything cognitive. For the stats the engine actually exposes, the usable numbers are the attention effect (g ≈ −0.78 under deprivation, g ≈ −0.41 under restriction) and the physical decrement, and that second one is the gift of this section: **−0.4 % of exercise performance per hour awake**, a linear dose term measured over a real range, which maps directly onto the endurance and combat surfaces. The pooled −7.56 % overall comes with I² = 98 %, so it should be quoted as a direction with a wide band and never as a precise multiplier. Two moderators deserve to survive into the design. First, **late restriction hurts and early restriction does not** — cutting the end of a sleep is what costs performance, which is a direct argument for the engine's `setForceWakeUpTime` path mattering and for a short sleep being penalised by *where* it was truncated. Second, **PM tasks are impaired and AM tasks largely are not**, which is a circadian interaction and the strongest empirical reason in this report to give the fatigue model a time-of-day term rather than a pure homeostatic one. Finally, the alcohol equivalence is the best available calibration anchor for the `TIRED` moodle: 17–19 h awake is worth BAC 0.05 %, and longer is worth BAC 0.10 % — a legally intoxicated character — which says vanilla's first Tired threshold firing somewhere around a normal bedtime is about right and that the top threshold should represent genuine incapacity rather than mild annoyance.

**Interpretation for a game model.** Only three nutritional levers in this table survive as fatigue mechanics with real numbers behind them, and they are not the ones a mod would reach for first. **Iron without anaemia is the strongest**: two independent meta-analyses, both with I² = 0 %, agree on a small-to-moderate effect on *subjective* fatigue (ES 0.33 and SMD −0.38) and agree equally firmly that **objective physical capacity does not improve** — so iron depletion should raise the mod's fatigue accrual and worsen the Tired moodle while leaving raw endurance capacity alone, which is an unusually specific and well-supported asymmetry. **Mild dehydration is the second**, and it is the fastest-acting: at only 1.4–1.6 % body-mass loss, before any measurable cognitive damage, mood and fatigue scores move in both sexes, and the cognitive effect only arrives past 2 % (ES −0.28). So dehydration belongs on the fatigue and unhappiness channels immediately and on the endurance channel only past the 2 % threshold, which is exactly the dehydration ruling the design already took (§ 4.4, mild from 2 % steepening toward 4 %) — this section supports the *mood* half starting earlier than that. **Energy deficit is the third**, but it is a slow one: the only hard threshold available is Loucks's ~30 kcal·kg LBM⁻¹·d⁻¹, and it was measured on reproductive endocrinology rather than on tiredness, so using it as a fatigue trigger is an extrapolation and must be labelled one; the direct fatigue evidence for energy deficit is Minnesota's qualitative symptom list (apathy, extreme weakness, irritability at >25 % weight loss) and the REDs consensus naming fatigue as an early sign. Two proposed levers should be **dropped or heavily discounted**: vitamin D, whose only fatigue meta-analysis is multiple-sclerosis-specific with an SMD of −0.18 whose confidence interval nearly touches zero, and B12, where 16 RCTs in 6276 people find supplementation ineffective — in both cases the honest mod rule is that *clinical deficiency* carries the effect through its anaemia or myopathy, and that topping up a replete character does nothing. Protein has no fatigue evidence at all, which is awkward because the engine already ships a protein branch in `getRecoveryMod`; using it is defensible as a game choice but must not be dressed as evidence. The high-glycaemic finding is a genuine and charming mechanic: a high-GI meal about four hours before bed halves sleep-onset latency (9 min vs 17.5 min), while a low-GI evening buys deeper N3 sleep — so a bowl of white rice should let a character fall asleep faster and a lentil stew should make the sleep worth more, which is a real trade-off that the evidence, unusually, supports in both directions. Note the caveat the primary studies themselves raise: the effect depends on the meal being *hours* before bed, and eating immediately before sleep does not reproduce it.

**Interpretation for a game model.** Caffeine is the best-quantified substance in this whole report and it hands the design a *pharmacokinetic term with a real constant*: a 5 h half-life in men, roughly doubling in women on oral contraceptives, driven by a CYP1A2 polymorphism that makes wide individual variation physiologically legitimate rather than a fudge — so a mod trait for fast and slow metabolisers is evidence-backed, not invented. On the wakefulness side the effects are large and the largest of them are exactly the ones a survival game cares about: after sleep loss, caffeine restores reaction time at g = 1.11 and driving control at g ≈ 1.6, and under operational conditions vigilance improves at SMD 0.73. That is strong enough to justify caffeine substantially offsetting the Tired moodle's penalties while the drug is on board — but it is **offsetting, not repaying**, and the two meta-analyses of caffeine's effect on subsequent sleep say precisely what the cost is: **45 minutes of total sleep time, 7 % of sleep efficiency, 9 minutes added to sleep onset and 11 minutes of deep sleep traded for light sleep.** Chang's independent replication lands slightly smaller (−34.7 min TST, −4.7 % efficiency) which is the right band of uncertainty to carry. The deep-sleep loss is the mechanically interesting part: in two-process terms, N3 is where homeostatic pressure is discharged, so caffeine should reduce the *rate* at which the mod's sleep recovery clears fatigue, not merely delay the start of sleep. Timing is the lever the design should actually expose, because it is quantified twice over: Drake's trial shows that even **6 hours** before bed a 400 mg dose disrupts sleep, and Gardiner's model puts the safe latency for a single mug of coffee at **8.8 hours**. A game with a compressed day length will need to scale those, and § 7 says how. Habituation and withdrawal are the last piece and they are unusually well pinned: **100 mg/day is enough to habituate**, withdrawal begins at **12–24 h**, peaks at **20–51 h** and lasts **2–9 days**, with headache in half of people and functional impairment in one in eight — a coffee-drinking character cut off from coffee should get a two-to-nine-day fatigue and concentration penalty, and that is a genuinely good survival mechanic that costs nothing to justify. Tolerance is the one soft spot: it is demonstrated for subjective effects after two weeks at 400 mg/day but **no decay rate for tolerance was recoverable**, so the design's tolerance-decay constant (§ 4.4 of the spec) remains a game judgement.

**Interpretation for a game model.** Alcohol's signature is a **two-halves-of-the-night** shape and that is the whole design: it shortens sleep onset and consolidates the first half, then fragments the second half — so a drunk character falls asleep fast and wakes badly, which is both physiologically right and mechanically satisfying. The modern meta-analysis is unusually honest about what is *not* known: REM disruption starts from a low dose (≤ 0.50 g·kg⁻¹, about two drinks for an average adult) and worsens with dose, but **total sleep time, sleep efficiency and wake-after-sleep-onset carry too much uncertainty to give a number**, so the mod must not invent one. What it can safely do is apply a **sleep-quality multiplier that falls with dose and is weighted toward the back half of the sleep**, and support it with the two mechanisms that are quantified: apnoea-hypopnoea events rise by about 2.3 per hour, and HRV falls dose-dependently, both of which say the second half of the night recovers less. The next-day cost is the clearest-cut part of the section and it is measured *after the alcohol has cleared* (BAC < 0.02 %): psychomotor speed g = 0.66, short-term memory g = 0.64, sustained attention g = 0.47 — so a hangover deserves a real, several-hour penalty on the same surfaces fatigue touches, applied with the intoxication stat already back at zero. The endurance finding sharpens that usefully: hangover subjects felt significantly more exhausted after a long walk but **completed it in the same time**, which argues for a perceived-exertion and fatigue-accrual penalty rather than a raw capacity penalty — the same asymmetry iron deficiency showed in § 3. The interaction with sleep debt is the most important line here for a game where players drink *because* they are tired: 4 h of sleep plus a BAC of only 0.035 g/dL degraded driving more than either insult alone, so the model should make alcohol and fatigue **super-additive** rather than simply summing. Finally, the chronic arm is weak evidence and should stay small: general drinking carries OR 1.37 for incident sleep disorder while the heavy-drinking estimate is not even significant, so a long-term alcoholism penalty on sleep is defensible in direction and not in magnitude, and Abrahao's point that alcohol sedation is *qualitatively distinct from sleep* is the right warning against letting a drunk character bank real recovery.

**Interpretation for a game model.** The literature's overall verdict is the *opposite* of the folk rule: exercise is good for sleep, including evening exercise, and the only reliably harmful case is vigorous exercise finishing within about an hour of bedtime. Stutz's pooled estimate has evening exercise **adding** 1.3 percentage points of slow-wave sleep and pushing REM later, which in two-process terms means a hard day's work both raises the homeostatic pressure and makes the subsequent sleep better at discharging it — so the design should let exertion feed fatigue accrual *and* raise the value of the sleep that follows, rather than treating exertion as a pure penalty. The one strong caveat is that the biggest dataset in this section, four million person-nights of wearables, does find later and harder exercise associated with delayed onset, shorter and worse sleep and worse nocturnal cardiac recovery — but it is observational, and the trials disagree, with Pajareya's pooled sleep-latency estimate a flat null (−2.2 min, CI spanning ±10 min). The reconciliation the evidence supports is a **timing threshold, not a magnitude**: Leota's four-hour rule and Stutz's one-hour rule bracket it, and a model that applies no penalty for exertion finishing more than roughly four game-hours before sleep and a growing penalty inside the last hour will be consistent with both. Intensity matters at the top end only: high-intensity evening work costs about 2 % of REM, moderate work costs nothing and may help. For the game's real signature case — a character who walks, fights and hauls for eighteen hours and then tries to sleep four — the ultra-endurance field data is the closest analogue available and it says two useful things. First, people in that state report **hallucinations and loss of balance**, which is an evidence-backed licence for the mod to express extreme fatigue through perception and stagger rather than only through a moodle icon. Second, and more usefully, **20-minute naps immediately reduced sleepiness** and finishers ran on under half an hour of sleep, so the model should let very short sleeps pay real, if partial, dividends — which matters because Project Zomboid's sleep is an interruptible timed action and a player who grabs ninety minutes should not get nothing.

### 7.1 A two-process-inspired core

Integrate in **game hours** (`Δgame-minutes / 60`), not real hours, so the `DayLength` sandbox option changes the wall-clock pace and not the physiology. Keep the homeostatic variable `S` in the mod's own modData and write a derived `F` into the engine stat.

| parameter | value / equation | rests on | grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| homeostatic build-up, awake | `dS/dt = (1 − S) / χw_eff` | χw = 18.2 h, Daan/Beersma/Borbély 1984 via Skeldon & Dijk 2025 | MODEL |
| homeostatic decay, asleep | `dS/dt = −S / χs_eff` | χs = 4.2 h, same | MODEL |
| baseline constants | `χw = 18.2` game-h, `χs = 4.2` game-h | same | MODEL |
| circadian term written into the engine stat | `F = clamp(S + a·cos(2π(h − 4)/24) − caffeine_offset + debt_visible, 0, 1)` with `a = 0.12`, `h = getTimeOfDay()` | amplitude 0.12 from the model; the 04:00 phase is a **game judgement** (the alertness trough); the *existence* of a time-of-day term rests on Craven 2022's PM-vs-AM asymmetry | MODEL + judgement |
| why `F = S` and not an affine remap | the model's own upper sleep threshold is **H⁺ = 0.67** and vanilla's first Tired level fires at **0.6**; the model's lower threshold **H⁻ = 0.17** is a plausible post-sleep floor | Skeldon & Dijk 2025; jar `MoodleStat.<clinit>` | MODEL |
| what the exponential replaces | vanilla's two-rate linear removal (top 0.7 over a nominal 5 h, last 0.3 over 7 h) is a piecewise-linear approximation of `exp(−t/4.2)`; the model's own `Tsleep = 4.2·ln(0.67/0.17) ≈ 5.8 h` sits inside vanilla's 5–7 h band | Skeldon & Dijk 2025; jar § C | MODEL |

**The threshold check, and it is the strongest result in this section.** Starting from `S = 0.17` after a full sleep, `S(t) = 1 − 0.83·exp(−t/18.2)` reaches vanilla's four Tired thresholds at:

| Tired level | threshold on `F` | hours awake | what the evidence says happens there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.6 | **13.3 h** | mid-evening; ordinary sleepiness |
| 2 | 0.7 | **18.5 h** | **Williamson & Feyer: 17–19 h awake ≈ BAC 0.05 %, response speeds up to 50 % slower** |
| 3 | 0.8 | **25.9 h** | past a full night lost; beyond the BAC-0.10 % equivalent |
| 4 | 0.9 | **38.5 h** | a day and a half; the ultra-endurance symptom set (hallucinations, loss of balance) |

Level 2 lands on the measured alcohol-equivalence point to within the width of the published window. **The recommendation is therefore to leave vanilla's thresholds entirely alone and change only the dynamics** — which is also the only option, since `MoodleStat` is not exposed to Lua.

A second consequence worth stating: the exponential recovery front-loads. From `S = 0.7`, a 90-minute nap gives `0.7·exp(−1.5/4.2) = 0.49` — a drop of 0.21, across a whole Tired level. That reproduces the ultramarathon finding that 20-minute naps immediately cut sleepiness and that finishers ran on under half an hour of sleep, and it does so for free out of the model's shape rather than by a special case.

### 7.6 Where each term is written

| term | engine seat | cadence |
|---|---|---|
| `S`, `D`, caffeine doses, alcohol doses, tolerance `T` | mod global modData, keyed by username | slow clock, per game-minute |
| `F` | `Stats.set(FATIGUE, …)` from the takeover handler | fast clock, per tick |
| accrual and recovery multipliers `m`, `r` | the coefficient set the slow clock leaves for the fast clock (§ 4.5 stat-coefficients channel) | rebuilt on grade change |
| sleep-onset latency | `setDelayToSleep` | on sleep start |
| endurance regen credit/penalty | recomputed inside the takeover handler (there is no `setRecoveryMod`) | per tick |
| unhappiness, stress, boredom from mood terms | the same coefficient set | per tick |
| level-4 severe expression | the § 4.5 event-driven channel; **do not add penalties at Tired 3–4** — vanilla already multiplies stomp power by 0.1 and 0.05 there, harsher than anything the human literature measures | on the event |

**Interpretation.** The single most useful thing this mapping produces is a reason **not** to change something: vanilla's Tired thresholds, read through the two-process constants, put level 2 at 18.5 hours awake, which is exactly where the alcohol-equivalence literature puts BAC 0.05 %, and `MoodleStat` is not exposed anyway. So the whole intervention is in the dynamics — swap vanilla's two-rate linear sleep removal for the true exponential with χs = 4.2 h, swap its flat accrual for the saturating exponential with χw = 18.2 h, add a circadian term off `getTimeOfDay()` and a slow debt pool, and hang the nutritional and substance multipliers on the two rate constants. Three properties fall out of that shape for free rather than being special-cased: short naps pay real dividends (the exponential front-loads), a sleep debt makes every subsequent day worse (χw shrinks), and caffeine masks without repaying (it moves `F`, never `S`). The λ = 0.30 convention is the design's biggest unearned assumption and should be reviewed as one: it is anchored on the engine's own sleep-trait span rather than on any measurement linking an SMD on a fatigue questionnaire to a rate of sleep-pressure accumulation, and no such measurement was found. Two proposed levers should be dropped outright — vitamin D and B12 as *fatigue* modifiers — and one, protein, should ship at 1.00 with a dial and an honest label, because the engine offers a seat for it and the literature offers nothing. The alcohol net-overnight multiplier is deliberately left as a bounded judgement rather than a number, because the 2025 meta-analysis says in as many words that total sleep time, sleep efficiency and wake-after-sleep-onset carry too much uncertainty to quote.

## Proposed model

A compact consolidated statement of § 7, in the form the kernel would take. **Every line is a proposal.** All times are **game hours**; all state is per player in the mod's global modData; nothing here is measured.

### State

```
S        homeostatic sleep pressure, [0,1]          -- the mod owns it; the engine never sees it
D        sleep debt, hours owed, [0,40]
caff[]   {mg, t}  doses with getWorldAgeHours() timestamps
alc[]    {g_per_kg, t}
T        caffeine tolerance, [0,1]
X        the day's exertion, MET-hours (from design 4.3)
```

### Per-minute update (slow clock)

```
h    = getGameTime():getTimeOfDay()                     -- hours of day, float
m    = accrual_multiplier(grades, hydration, EA, withdrawal)     -- 7.2, 7.3
r    = recovery_multiplier(bed, caffeine, alcohol, meal, X, heat) -- 7.2, 7.3, 7.4
Xw   = 18.2 / (m * (1 + 0.02*D))
Xs   = 4.2  * bedLadder * sleepTraits * r                -- bedLadder is vanilla's 0.60..1.15
```

### Per-tick update (fast clock, takeover handler)

```
dt = game_seconds_this_tick / 3600
if asleep and timeOfSleep > delayToActuallySleep then
    S = max(0.01*D, S - S/Xs * dt)                       -- exponential decay, floored by debt
else
    S = S + (1 - S)/Xw * dt                              -- saturating exponential rise
end
C       = sum over caff of mg * 2^(-(now - t)/5.0)       -- 9.7 for a slow metaboliser
offset  = 0.35 * C/(C + 150) * (1 - 0.6*T)
F       = clamp(S + 0.12*cos(2*pi*(h - 4)/24) + 0.005*D - offset, 0, 1)
Stats:set(FATIGUE, F)
```

### Three properties that fall out of the shape rather than being special-cased

1. **Naps pay.** From `S = 0.7`, ninety minutes gives `S = 0.49` — a whole Tired level — because `exp(−t/4.2)` front-loads. That reproduces Bourlois 2026's 20-minute-nap finding without a special case.
2. **Debt compounds.** `χw` shrinks as `D` grows, so each short night makes the next day accrue faster, reproducing Van Dongen 2003's cumulative dose-response.
3. **Caffeine masks without repaying.** It enters `F` and never `S`, so the debt keeps growing behind the mask — which is what Sigmon 2009's "no net beneficial effects of chronic administration" implies.

### Hard prerequisites

- The server must have **both** `SleepAllowed` and `SleepNeeded` true, or `Stats.reset(FATIGUE)` wipes `F` every tick before the Lua hook can run (jar § C). With either false, the mod keeps `S` internally and there is **no Tired moodle at all**; the mod should detect this and say so rather than silently doing nothing.
- Integration must be in game time, or `DayLength` silently rescales the physiology.
- `MoodleStat` is not in the exposer set, so the thresholds are immovable — which § 7.1 argues is fortunate.

## Gaps

**Method limitation, stated first because it shapes everything below.** The session's WebSearch budget (200 calls) was exhausted before this report began, so every source here was discovered and verified through the Europe PMC REST search API alone. That API matches on title and abstract fields, so a relevant paper with an oblique title is invisible to it, and no Google Scholar, PubMed-native or publisher-site search was run. Two publisher fetches were refused outright (Wiley `onlinelibrary.wiley.com` for the Borbély 2016 reappraisal, and `efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com` for the EFSA caffeine opinion, both HTTP 403), and one Europe PMC full text failed (PMC4434546, HTTP 500).

Unverified or missing, by section:

1. **Sleep need and debt.**
   - The AASM/SRS consensus figure ("7 or more hours") could not be read: the record is verified but the PMC full text returned HTTP 500, and the abstract does not state the number. The NSF 7–9 h band (Hirshkowitz 2015) is used instead, and it *is* verified.
   - The Borbély 2016 reappraisal's own text was never read (Wiley 403). The τ values are taken from Skeldon & Dijk's 2025 open-access restatement, which attributes them to Daan/Beersma/Borbély 1984; **the primary 1984 paper's numbers were not read directly.** That is a second-hand chain and should be closed by reading either paper.
   - **Sleep environment is the weakest area in the report.** No effect size exists for sleeping surface (Radwan 2015 gives a qualitative ranking only), so vanilla's bed ladder (0.60 floor to 1.15 good bed + pillow) has **no evidentiary support for its magnitudes at all** — only for its ordering. Noise and light were not searched.
   - No meta-analysis of recovery-sleep dynamics *across multiple nights* was found; Banks 2010 and Jones 2024 both test one recovery night.

2. **Sleep loss on performance.**
   - Pilcher & Huffcutt 1996's per-domain effect sizes are not in the abstract, so only their *ordering* (mood > cognitive > motor) is usable.
   - Dawson & Reid 1997's abstract was not fetchable; Williamson & Feyer 2000 carries the equivalence numbers instead.
   - No meta-analysis specific to **strength** under sleep loss was found; Craven 2022 pools 66 strength measures inside an overall estimate with I² = 98 %, so a strength-specific number is unavailable.
   - No dose-response curve for cognition against *hours of sleep obtained* (as opposed to hours awake) was found.

3. **Nutrition and fatigue.**
   - **Protein and fatigue: nothing.** No trial or review was located. This matters because the engine ships a protein branch in `getRecoveryMod` that the design would like to use.
   - **No general-population vitamin D and fatigue meta-analysis** was found — only the multiple-sclerosis one, whose CI touches zero.
   - Markun 2021's **fatigue-specific** pooled estimate was not recovered; only the cognition and depression arms were quoted. The title covers fatigue, so the number exists and should be read from the full text.
   - No quantitative link exists anywhere in this report between an energy deficit and a *rate* of fatigue accrual. Loucks's 30 kcal·kg LBM⁻¹·d⁻¹ threshold is a reproductive endpoint, and using it for fatigue is an extrapolation the design must keep labelled.
   - Iron: both meta-analyses measure *supplementation of the deficient*, not *progressive depletion of the replete*, so the timescale over which iron depletion produces fatigue is unmeasured.

4. **Caffeine.**
   - **No tolerance decay rate exists in any source found.** The design's `τ_down` is pure judgement.
   - Caffeine's dose-response on alertness in **non-sleep-deprived** people was not found as a meta-analysis; Irwin 2020 and Wang 2026 both condition on sleep loss.
   - EFSA's caffeine safety opinion (the natural source for a single-dose and daily-intake threshold, and for a half-life range) was refused. The 5.0 h half-life rests on a single trial's control arm (Haller 2004) rather than on a pharmacokinetic review.
   - Habitual-user differences in the *alertness* response (as opposed to the subjective one) were not quantified.
   - Tea-specific and evening-exercise-plus-caffeine numbers exist (Payne 2025, Kocak 2025) but were not recovered.

5. **Alcohol.**
   - **Total sleep time, sleep efficiency and wake-after-sleep-onset have no usable pooled estimate** — Gardiner 2025 says so explicitly. The design's net-overnight bound of [0.80, 1.00] is judgement.
   - Hangover *duration* as a function of dose was not found; the design's `H` is unparameterised.
   - The super-additivity of alcohol and sleep debt rests on one small driving-simulator study (Vakulin 2007, young men only). `γ = 0.5` is invented.
   - Chronic alcohol effects on sleep architecture were not searched beyond the incident-sleep-disorder cohort meta-analysis.

6. **Exercise and sleep.**
   - Kredlow 2015's numeric Hedges' g values are not in the abstract; only magnitude labels ("small", "moderate") were recovered.
   - Souabni 2026's SMD **sign convention could not be resolved** from the abstract — the reported −0.51 is described in text as "higher WASO".
   - Leota 2025's minute-level effects are not in the abstract, only the four-hour threshold and the directions.
   - No study quantifies how **prolonged exertion raises the rate of fatigue accrual**; Stutz's +1.3 pp SWS is the closest proxy and it measures the following sleep, not the accrual. The ultra-endurance literature is observational, survivor-biased and tiny.

7. **Engine mapping.**
   - **`λ = 0.30` is the design's largest unearned assumption.** No measurement anywhere links an SMD on a fatigue questionnaire to a rate of homeostatic sleep-pressure accumulation. The whole nutritional-multiplier table inherits that uncertainty.
   - The circadian **phase** (04:00 trough) is a judgement; only the amplitude (0.12) and the existence of a time-of-day effect are evidenced.
   - Whether the takeover handler reproduces vanilla's fatigue path bit-close on a live server is unmeasured, and is already a named experiment in the design spec § 8.
   - Whether a 90-minute in-game sleep actually reaches `timeOfSleep > delayToActuallySleep` and so pays anything is a live-server question, not a literature one.
   - `O_max = 0.35` for caffeine is derived by comparing two meta-analyses with different outcome scales (Irwin's g against Lim & Dinges's g), which is not a licensed operation and should be treated as calibration, not inference.
