# Cognition, mood and perception: evidence base for a realism nutrition model

Scope: vision and dark adaptation, hearing, reaction time and attention, mood, stress and anxiety — the nutritional, hydration, sleep, caffeine and alcohol determinants of each, with effect sizes. Audience: the design of a Project Zomboid mod that owns the fatigue and intoxication stats (which the client's vision cone and hearing range read), the stress, panic, unhappiness and boredom stats, the night-vision and short-sighted trait toggles, and the aiming delay.

## How to read this

Every row carries a grade for the kind of evidence behind it:

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| MA | meta-analysis or systematic review of human studies |
| RCT | randomised controlled trial, randomised crossover, or a controlled clamp/laboratory protocol with randomised order |
| COH | cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, case series, or a controlled human depletion/repletion experiment that is not randomised |
| AUTH | authoritative report or consensus statement — WHO, IOM/NASEM DRI, EFSA, a Cochrane diagnostic-accuracy review |
| TXT | textbook, narrative review, or expert consensus without a systematic search |

Five conventions matter for reading this report.

**Every citation was verified by fetching its record.** Every DOI and PMID below was confirmed against the Europe PMC REST API (`ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search`), and the title, author list, year and journal were matched against what is written here. Numbers quoted in a row come from the abstract text that fetch returned, and where a figure could be traced only to a secondary source the row names the source that was fetched. Where a number could not be confirmed, the row says **not verified in this run** and the number is not used to support any design decision.

**The session's web-search budget was exhausted before this report began**, so every source here was found through the Europe PMC search API used directly as a search engine — mostly `TITLE:` field queries, because Europe PMC's free-text relevance ranking is strongly recency-biased and its `sort=CITED` is not query-scoped. That biases discovery toward papers whose *titles* contain the search terms. A well-known paper with an oblique title may therefore be missing, and `## Gaps` names the specific searches that could not be run.

**Two of the brief's named papers were not reachable and are not cited as sources.** Keys 1950, *The Biology of Human Starvation*, is a book with no DOI or PMID; its content is taken from a verified secondary account, exactly as the energy report does. Lim & Dinges 2010 *was* reachable and is used directly. Where the brief named a paper by author and year and the record that came back did not match that description, the row says so.

**The direction of evidence matters more here than anywhere else in the library.** Most of the modern meta-analytic literature on nutrients and cognition or mood tests *supplementing people who are already replete*, and mostly finds nothing or finds an effect confined to a deficient subgroup. A survival game cares about the opposite direction — what happens when intake stops. So the depletion, deprivation and clamp studies carry the weight below, and the supplementation meta-analyses appear mainly to mark where a proposed mechanic is **unsupported**. § 5 lists the mechanics that should be omitted for exactly this reason, and that list is as much of the deliverable as the ones that should be built.

**Units and the engine's scale.** The engine surfaces this report can move are: the fatigue stat `F` and the intoxication stat `I`, both `[0,1]`-ish and both read by the client's vision cone and hearing range; the stress, panic, unhappiness and boredom stats; the `NIGHT_VISION` and `SHORT_SIGHTED` trait booleans; and `setAimingDelay`, a float in the same units as the weapon's `aimingTime`. Throughout, a **coefficient** is a multiplier applied to one of those, and an **offset** is added to one. The jar report (`jar-perception-speed.md`) is the authority on what each surface does; this report is the authority on how large the effect should be. Where the two disagree about what is reachable, the jar report wins.

## 1. Vision

Throughout this section a **log unit** is a factor of ten in the light a subject needs before they can detect it. A 1.0 log-unit rise in dark-adapted threshold means the character needs ten times the light; 0.3 log units means twice the light. This is the natural currency for a night-blindness mechanic, and § 5 maps it onto the engine's cone degrees.

Vitamin A is the one nutrient in this whole report whose deficiency maps onto a *sensory* game mechanic with real quantitative backing, and the mapping is unusually clean. The engine's night-vision trait adds `36 × (1 − dayLightStrength)` degrees, worth everything at midnight and nothing at noon; vitamin A deficiency selectively attenuates dark-adapted responses and leaves light-adapted ones untouched, which is the same shape for the same reason — free opsin desensitises rods and does not desensitise cones (Margetts 2025). So a mod that grants and withdraws that trait on vitamin A status is not inventing an effect, it is reproducing the one the physiology actually has. The magnitudes are also usable: a deficient adult sits roughly **0.3 to 1.1 log units** worse than a replete one, recovers **0.3 log units** within days of a large dose, and needs about **six weeks at an adequate dietary intake** to recover **0.7 log units** — so onset and recovery are both week-scale, not hour-scale, and the game's onset dial is what compresses that to a playable span.

Three details are worth building in because they are cheap and they are measured. First, **75 % of affected adults did not know they were affected** (Abbott-Johnson 2011), which is an argument for making the penalty visible only through play — a darker screen — rather than through a moodle that announces itself. Second, **β-carotene did not work** where preformed retinol did (Congdon 2000), so a food pass that treats plant and animal vitamin A as interchangeable will overstate the player's recovery; the retinol-activity-equivalent conversion the item pass already uses is doing real work here. Third, **iron and riboflavin gate the response to vitamin A** (Graham 2007): an iron-deficient character should recover night vision more slowly even on adequate vitamin A, which is a genuine, cited interaction for the § 4.4 interaction factors rather than an invented one.

Zinc deserves a secondary role and no more. The evidence that zinc deficiency causes night blindness independently of vitamin A is real but thin — six cirrhotics and three uncontrolled responders (Morrison 1978) plus a single case (Mochizuki 2006) — and every subject was a patient with confounding liver disease or malabsorption. It is enough to justify zinc as a *second gate* on the same night-vision trait, so that a character replete in vitamin A but depleted in zinc still loses it; it is not enough to justify a separate zinc perception ladder with its own numbers.

The two negatives are as useful as the positives. **Dehydration has no measurable effect on visual acuity or contrast sensitivity** in anything reachable — the literature is about intraocular pressure and tear films, which no player will ever perceive — so a thirst-to-vision penalty should not be built. And **hypoglycaemia's effect is cognitive, not sensory**: it degrades attention and processing speed, which belong in § 3 and reach the game through aiming delay, not through the cone. Lutein is real but slow (8–16 weeks) and its outcome is daylight contrast, which the engine's cone does not model at all; it is a candidate for a flavour line in the panel and not for a coefficient.

Hearing is the weakest chapter in this report, and the honest conclusion is that **nutrition should not drive the mod's hearing range at all**. Every effect that exists is chronic, decade-scale and measured in single decibels. The folate RCT — the highest-quality evidence in the whole section — bought **0.7 dB over three years**, at the low frequencies only, in a population selected for raised homocysteine in a country without fortification (Durga 2007). A 0.7 dB difference is below what a listener notices in a clinical booth, let alone what a player would notice in a zombie game. The iron-deficiency cohort looks more impressive at HR 1.50 until the absolute numbers are read: 0.98 % versus 0.81 % over five years (Chen 2025). Scaled to a character surviving a few game months, that is nothing.

The contrast with the engine's own numbers makes the point sharply. `IsoGameCharacter.getDetectionRange` starts at 3.5 tiles and subtracts the raw fatigue stat, so a fully fatigued character loses **1.0 of 3.5 tiles — a 29 % cut in hearing radius**. Nothing in the nutritional literature comes within two orders of magnitude of justifying a swing that size on a game timescale. If the mod wires a micronutrient grade to hearing range, it will be inventing an effect, and it will be inventing a large one.

That leaves exactly one defensible route, and it is already wired: **the fatigue stat**. The engine subtracts fatigue from detection range for free, the mod owns fatigue, and the sleep-loss literature does support a real attentional decrement (§ 3). So hearing degradation in this mod should be a consequence of sleep debt and energy availability moving fatigue, with no nutrient-specific hearing term anywhere in the effects table. Iron is the one possible exception worth *labelling* rather than *coefficient-ing*: a clinically anaemic character could carry a small flat detection-range penalty on the mechanistic grounds Ye 2024 gives, declared in the register as a game choice resting on a cohort with a 0.17-percentage-point absolute effect — which is to say, declared honestly as a liberty.

Two further details are worth carrying for their own sake. **Vitamin C at high supplemental doses was associated with *worse* hearing** (RR 1.22 for ≥1000 mg/d; Curhan 2015), which is a reminder that the excess ladders § 4.4 builds are not decoration. And **β-carotene was protective for hearing** (RR 0.88) in the same cohort where it was *useless* for dark adaptation (§ 1, Congdon 2000) — a good illustration of why a mod cannot treat one carotenoid number as one biological effect.

This section contains the report's most important negative and its most important positive, and they are about the same thing.

The negative is **acute fasting does nothing measurable to cognition**. Bamberg & Moreau pooled 222 effect sizes over 3484 participants at a median 12 h fast and got **g = 0.02 with a credible interval spanning zero and low heterogeneity** — this is not an underpowered null, it is a well-estimated zero. So the intuitive mechanic "hungry character reacts slower" has no support at all, and a mod that wires the hunger bar to reaction time will be fabricating the effect. What *does* impair cognition is **actual hypoglycaemia**, at 2.6–3.0 mmol/L (Warren & Frier 2005), and at 3.0 mmol/L the magnitude is large — **−0.7 SD globally** (Nilsson 2019). The design consequence is precise: the reaction penalty must hang off the model's *blood-glucose or energy-availability* state deep into glycogen depletion and starvation, not off hunger, and a character who has merely skipped a day's meals should get **no penalty whatsoever**. That single distinction is worth more to this mod than any coefficient in the table.

The positive is that sleep loss and alcohol are **measured against each other in the same units**, which is exactly what the engine needs, because `LightingJNI.calculateVisionCone` reads the fatigue stat *and* the intoxication stat in one formula. Twenty-four hours awake sits at **0.05 % BAC** by the variability-corrected metric and under **0.08 %** by the crude one (Falleti 2003; Maruff 2005), and the alcohol side has its own graded band structure — 0.03 % touching one measure, 0.05 % two, 0.08 % nearly all (Yadav 2019). So the mod can pick one internal impairment scale, calibrate sleep debt onto it at 24 h ≈ 0.05 % BAC-equivalent, and drive both engine stats from it without inventing a second scale. Dehydration slots onto the same ruler: **attention ES −0.52** is the largest dehydration domain effect and it is roughly two-thirds of sleep deprivation's **−0.776**, with the knee at **>2 % body-mass loss** — which is the same 2 % threshold the energy report's endurance ruling already uses.

Caffeine is the one intervention in this report with a clean, large, well-replicated benefit, and its size depends entirely on state. In rested people it is **g ≈ 0.28** on reaction time (Kløve & Petersen 2025) — barely worth modelling. In sleep-deprived people the same drug reaches **g = 1.11 to 1.95** on information-processing speed (Irwin 2020). So caffeine's coefficient in the mod must be *multiplicative on the sleep-debt penalty* rather than an additive bonus, or a well-rested character will get a stimulant effect the literature says they do not get.

Then there is Tikuisis 2004, which is the single most directly applicable paper in this report. Sleep deprivation degraded both halves of a rifle task; caffeine **restored target engagement time and rate of fire but not accuracy or precision**. The authors split it as cognitive-detection versus psychomotor-marksmanship. The engine happens to split the same way: `setAimingDelay` governs how long before the shot is good, while the hit roll governs whether it lands. So the mapping writes itself — sleep debt should raise aiming delay *and* worsen the shot, while caffeine should cut aiming delay *without* improving the shot. That is a genuinely evidence-derived asymmetry rather than a balance guess, and § 5 builds it in.

The micronutrient rows are, with one exception, a list of things not to build. **B12 does nothing** to cognition in people without overt deficiency across 16 RCTs and 6276 subjects (Markun 2021). **Iron's cognitive effect vanishes when iron-deficient participants are excluded** (Fiani 2025), which is the cleanest possible statement that iron matters only as a deficiency and never as a top-up — and it fits the ladder structure § 4.4 already uses. The exception is iron's **attention** effect at **SMD 0.59** (Falkingham 2010), which is large, heterogeneity-free, and the best case in the whole report for a micronutrient touching reaction time; it deserves a real coefficient at the depleted and clinical grades. Omega-3's numbers are too large to believe at GRADE low, and glucose's effect is on memory rather than reaction time and explicitly **not on mood** — so neither earns a perception or reaction coefficient, and § 5 omits both.

Mood is where this mod's four stats — stress, panic, unhappiness, boredom — get their numbers, and the evidence sorts cleanly into three tiers.

**Tier one, large and trustworthy: sleep loss, dehydration and exercise.** Palmer 2024 is the strongest single result in the whole report: 1338 effect sizes over 154 studies, with **positive affect down by SMD −0.27 to −1.14 under every form of sleep loss** and **anxiety up by SMD +0.57 to +0.63**. Note the asymmetry, because it is a real and repeatedly-found one — sleep loss reliably takes away good feeling and reliably adds anxiety, but its effect on *negative* affect and depressive symptoms "varied by sleep loss type" and was not uniform. For a game with separate unhappiness and panic stats that is a gift: sleep debt should drive **panic** (anxiety, SMD ≈ 0.6) hard and **unhappiness** (loss of positive affect) hard, and should *not* be modelled as a uniform misery multiplier. Dehydration lands in the same tier at a smaller size and, crucially, **mood moves before cognition does** — at just 1.4–1.6 % body-mass loss both sexes showed significant mood disturbance while "most cognitive performance aspects remained unaffected" (Armstrong 2012; Ganio 2011). Exercise is the largest evidence base here (79 551 participants) at **depression SMD −0.61 and anxiety SMD −0.47** (Munro 2026), which justifies a real mood credit for physical activity — and the finding that *lower*-intensity, *shorter* exercise did most for anxiety is worth respecting rather than inverting.

**Tier two, real but modest or dose-shaped: caffeine, alcohol, vitamin D, omega-3.** Caffeine is the only substance here with a clean anxiogenic signal and a usable threshold: **SMD 0.61 at low dose rising to 2.86 above roughly 400 mg** (Liu 2024). That gives caffeine a genuine two-sided character in the mod — it buys reaction time (§ 3) and it charges panic, with the charge accelerating past 400 mg. Alcohol's mood effect is real but only demonstrated for the **hangover**, not the intoxication (Rothman 2026), and the long-term anxiety literature is explicitly too inconsistent to use (D'Aquino 2024). So alcohol should raise stress and unhappiness *the morning after*, not during. Vitamin D's headline **SMD −0.32 per 1000 IU/d** is tempting but carries two warnings I would not ignore: the effect was **stronger in shorter trials**, which is the classic signature of small-study bias, and the 8000 IU estimate has a confidence interval 3.5 SD wide. Omega-3's **−0.28 overall, −0.50 for EPA-pure at ≤1 g/d, and nothing at all for DHA** (Liao 2019) is the most mechanistically specific result in the section, and it is the one row that argues for the mod distinguishing EPA from DHA rather than tracking one "omega-3" pool.

**Tier three, should not be built: magnesium, B12, B-vitamin anxiety effects, glucose, and the Minnesota numbers.** Boyle 2017 is routinely cited as evidence that magnesium reduces anxiety; read directly, it pooled nothing, found positive effects in only about half of each subgroup, and states that **no validated stress measure was administered in any included study**. That does not support a coefficient. B12 does nothing to depression across 6276 subjects (Markun 2021). B vitamins reach **SMD 0.23 for stress** but **0.03 for anxiety** (Young 2019), so at most a small stress term and certainly no panic term. Glucose explicitly did not move mood at any dose (Sünram-Lea 2011). And the Minnesota experiment — the study everyone reaches for when modelling starvation psychology — yields a **symptom list and not a single effect size**: apathy, irritability, weakness, fatigue. It is excellent evidence that severe energy deficit wrecks mood and useless for saying by how much. Iron sits awkwardly between tiers: **anxiety d = 0.34** is a real number, but the same paper reports that the effect **vanishes once iron-deficient participants are excluded** (Fiani 2025), which means iron belongs on the deficiency ladder and nowhere else.

## 5. Engine mapping (speculative)

**Everything in this section is labelled speculative and none of it is a claim.** The evidence in §§ 1–4 constrains the *ratios*; the absolute magnitudes are game choices, and every one of them is stated as such. The engine facts are the jar report's, not this report's. Nothing here has been run.

### 5.0 The unit, and the engine's own exchange rate

To keep the coefficients commensurable I define one **impairment unit (IU)** = the cognitive effect of 24 h total sleep deprivation, which is Lim & Dinges' **g = −0.776** on lapses in simple attention. Every other effect size in § 3 divides by 0.776 to give its size in IU:

The engine supplies its own exchange rate, and it is worth reading before choosing anything, because it tells you how the *game* values these surfaces. In `LightingJNI.calculateVisionCone`: fatigue costs **72° per unit** (and another 36° at maximum), full intoxication costs **36°**, panic level 4 costs **36°**, and the night-vision trait is worth **+36° at midnight and nothing at noon**. So the engine itself equates *one night-vision trait ≈ full intoxication ≈ panic level 4 ≈ half a unit of fatigue*. Fatigue is by far the strongest lever, which is convenient, since fatigue is also the stat with the best scientific backing.

The stat ranges that bound every coefficient below, from the health report: `FATIGUE` 0–1 (Tired moodle at 0.6/0.7/0.8/0.9), `INTOXICATION` 0–100 (Drunk moodle at 10/30/50/70), `STRESS` 0–1 (moodle 0.25/0.50/0.75/0.90), `PANIC` 0–100 (moodle 6/30/65/80), `UNHAPPINESS` 0–100 (moodle 20/45/60/80), `BOREDOM` 0–100 (moodle 25/50/75/90).

### 5.1 Which grades drive the fatigue-like cone and hearing penalties

The cone and the hearing range read **only** fatigue and intoxication. Writing to either has a side effect — the Tired and Drunk moodles, and for fatigue the whole sleep system — so a driver earns a fatigue write only if it is *genuinely fatigue-like*, meaning the literature measured a fatigue or somnolence construct and not merely an attention decrement. | Dehydration ≥2 % BML | **yes**, small | **+0.06 F** at 2 %, rising to **+0.10 F** at 4 % | This is the one hydration effect that *is* measured as fatigue: "fatigue-inertia" and total mood disturbance in women at −1.36 % (Armstrong 2012) and "fatigue increased at rest" in men at −1.59 % (Ganio 2011) |
| Hypoglycaemia | **no** | — | Measured as processing speed and attention, not fatigue (Nilsson 2019). Routes to aiming delay (§ 5.4) |
| Iron depletion | **no** | — | Attention, not fatigue-as-cone; and there is no evidence at all for *perceptual narrowing* from iron. Routes to aiming delay |
| Any micronutrient | **no** | — | No nutrient in §§ 1–4 has evidence for a visual-field or somnolence effect |
| Alcohol | **intoxication, not fatigue** | see § 5.4 | The engine already routes intoxication into both cone and hearing; the mod should write `INTOXICATION` and let the engine do the rest |
| **Hearing, any nutrient** | **no — omit entirely** | — | § 2: the best evidence is **0.7 dB over three years** (Durga 2007) and an absolute risk difference of **0.17 percentage points over five years** (Chen 2025). The engine's fatigue term already swings hearing by 29 %. No nutrient term is defensible |

The total fatigue offset from non-sleep sources should be **capped at +0.15**, hard, and the cap should be enforced so that offsets alone can never carry a well-rested character across the Tired moodle threshold at 0.6. Otherwise a dehydrated character is told to go to bed, and sleeping will "cure" thirst — a visible wrongness for a 0.06 gain.

### 5.2 The night-vision trait: when to grant and when to withdraw

`CharacterTrait.NIGHT_VISION` adds `36 × (1 − dayLightStrength)` degrees, so it is worth everything at midnight and nothing at noon. Vitamin A deficiency attenuates dark-adapted responses and leaves light-adapted ones untouched (Margetts 2025). The shapes match, which is why this is the one perception mechanic in the mod that is reproducing physiology rather than inventing it.

| rule | value | justification |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline (no trait) represents | **marginal** vitamin A status | The character starts where vanilla puts them; the trait is the *replete* reward, because the engine has no lever that pushes dark adaptation below its own baseline |
| Grant requires | vitamin A **replete** *and* zinc not **depleted**, held continuously for **14 game days** | Repletion took **6 weeks at 850 µg RAE/d** to recover 0.71 log units (Haskell 2005); 14 days is that compressed by the onset dial, and must be registered as the dial's doing. The zinc gate rests on Morrison 1978 and Mochizuki 2006 — thin, so it is a *gate*, not a second ladder |
| Withdraw at | first minute at **marginal** or below | Asymmetric on purpose: the acute-dose study got 0.3 log units back within days (Abbott-Johnson 2011), but decline is not measured, and a fast withdrawal is the conservative direction |
| Hysteresis | re-grant requires **3 game days** back at replete | `CharacterTraits.read` does a full `reset()`-then-`add()` replace of the client's trait map, so a flapping grant rewrites the whole map every tick. Hysteresis is a mechanism requirement, not a balance choice |
| Ownership flag | **never remove a natively-held Night Vision**; never re-add one the mod did not add | The spec already requires this, and `Nutrition.applyTraitFromWeight` shares the same trait map on its own schedule |
| β-carotene must not satisfy the grant | preformed retinol only, at full RAE weighting | β-carotene was **indistinguishable from placebo** for dark adaptation (Congdon 2000) while preformed retinol worked |
| Iron or riboflavin depletion | **slows** the grant timer (suggest ×2 the 14 days) | Iron-deficient women improved significantly more with iron + riboflavin added (Graham 2007); abnormal-threshold prevalence fell 87 % → 30 % |
| Sync | server writes the trait then calls `sendSyncPlayerFields(player, 2)` | Reaches **that player's own connection only** — which is fine, because the cone is computed client-side per player anyway. Other clients' copies keep a stale trait list, and that is harmless here |

One design note worth acting on: **75 % of affected adults did not know they were affected** (Abbott-Johnson 2011). That argues for granting and withdrawing this trait *silently* — no moodle, no toast — and letting the player discover it by finding the dark darker. It is the rare case where the realistic choice and the interesting choice agree.

### 5.3 When the Short Sighted toggle is justified

`hasTrait(SHORT_SIGHTED)` is the mod's only lever on view *distance*, and it is a blunt one: it reaches `LightingJNI.playerSet`, sets `blurFactorTarget` through `updateVisionEffects`, cuts `getAlphaUpdateRateMul`, and — the big one — makes `HandWeapon.getMaxSightRange` return the *minimum* sight range outright whenever the character is not wearing glasses. That last effect guts ranged combat.

| verdict | condition | justification |
|---|---|---|
| **Justified** | **clinical** vitamin A deficiency only, and only with the severity dial at or above its default | Clinical VAD produces corneal involvement — xerosis, Bitot's spots, keratomalacia — and **21 % of evaluated VAD patients had xerophthalmia signs with 18 % showing corneal findings** (Fearon 2026). Corneal disease degrades acuity, not just dark adaptation, so distance loss is the right shape |
| Justified, with a caveat | same, but **suppressed while the character wears glasses** | The engine already exempts glasses-wearers from the sight-range penalty, so the mod inherits a free and physiologically wrong exemption. Worth documenting rather than fighting |
| **Not justified** | dehydration | **No evidence** on acuity or contrast sensitivity at all (§ 1) |
| **Not justified** | hypoglycaemia | No visual-function evidence; the effect is cognitive (§ 1, § 3) |
| **Not justified** | fatigue or sleep debt | The cone already handles it at 72°/unit; adding distance loss would double-count |
| **Not justified** | zinc, lutein, omega-3, any B vitamin | Zinc's evidence is scotopic only; lutein's outcome is daylight contrast on a 16-week timescale (Machida 2020), which the cone does not model |

Because the weapon-sight consequence is so large, Short Sighted should be the **last** thing the ladder does, behind the same dial that gates lethality, and it should carry the same ownership flag as Night Vision.

### 5.4 The aiming-delay multiplier

`IsoGameCharacter.setAimingDelay` is public and read by `CombatManager.calculateHitChanceData` through `getAimDelayPenalty`, but `CombatManager.setAimingDelay` **recomputes it from the weapon after every shot**, so the mod must re-apply on the shot event. The value is clamped to `[0, aimingTime]`, so the multiplier operates inside that ceiling and saturates there.

```
IU_total = IU_sleep + IU_dehydration + IU_glucose + IU_iron + IU_alcohol
           - IU_caffeine                                   (floored, see below)

aimingDelayMult = clamp(0.90, 1.60, 1 + 0.25 * IU_total)

then:  setAimingDelay( min(aimingTime, baseAimingDelay * aimingDelayMult) )
```

The **0.25 coefficient is a game choice**: it makes one unit of impairment — 24 h awake, or 0.05 % BAC — cost 25 % more time to steady the weapon. Nothing in the literature fixes it; what the literature fixes is the *ratios between the terms*.

| term | value | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `IU_sleep` | the mod's sleep-debt hours mapped through the fatigue sibling's curve; **1.00 at 24 h continuous wakefulness**, cap 1.50 | Lim & Dinges 2010; the 24 h anchor is Falleti 2003 / Maruff 2005 |
| `IU_dehydration` | 0 below 1 % BML; `0.45 × (BML − 1)` for 1–2 %; `0.45 + 0.22 × (BML − 2)` for 2–4 %; cap **0.89** | Wittbrodt 2018 with the derived rescaling of § 5.0 — flag the derivation |
| `IU_glucose` | 0 above 3.5 mmol/L; linear ramp to **0.90** at 3.0 mmol/L; held at 0.90 below | Threshold 2.6–3.0 (Warren & Frier 2005); magnitude at 3.0 (Nilsson 2019). **Must be driven by modelled blood glucose, never by the hunger bar** |
| `IU_iron` | 0 replete and marginal; **0.38** depleted; **0.76** clinical | Falkingham 2010 SMD 0.59; the depleted value is half, a game choice |
| `IU_alcohol` | **0.30** at 0.03 % BAC-equivalent, **1.00** at 0.05 %, **1.60** at 0.08 %, linear between, cap 2.00 | Yadav 2019 bands; the 0.05 % ≡ 24 h anchor from Falleti/Maruff. Mapping BAC onto the engine's `INTOXICATION` 0–100 is **unmeasured**: if `I = 100` is taken as ≈0.20 % BAC then 0.05 % ≈ `I = 25` and 0.08 % ≈ `I = 40`, which is consistent with the Drunk moodle's 10/30/50/70 thresholds but is a **pure inference and must be registered as one** |
| `IU_caffeine` | `min(IU_sleep, 0.50 + 0.60 × min(1, dose_mg / 200))`, cap **0.50** when `IU_sleep ≈ 0` | Irwin 2020 (1.43 under sleep loss) vs Kløve 2025 (0.36 rested). The `min(IU_sleep, …)` is the important part: **caffeine's size is conditional on being impaired**, and a rested character must not get the sleep-deprived effect |

**Combination rules.**

1. **Penalties add.** No study measured two of these together, so additivity is the neutral assumption and must be registered as an assumption, not a finding. Cap `IU_total` at **2.50**.
2. **Caffeine subtracts but cannot invert.** The `min(IU_sleep, …)` term plus the `0.90` floor on the multiplier means caffeine can claw back a sleep-debt penalty and give a small rested bonus, but can never make a character better than a rested one by more than 10 %.
3. **Alcohol and sleep debt add, flagged.** Falleti and Maruff measured them as *equivalent*, never combined. Additive is a guess; say so.
4. **Tolerance.** Caffeine's dose-response was **unrelated to habitual consumption** (Kløve & Petersen 2025), which contradicts the intuitive tolerance mechanic. § 4.4's caffeine tolerance term should therefore affect the *duration* of the effect, not its peak, or be dropped.
5. **Re-apply after every shot**, because `CombatManager.setAimingDelay` overwrites it.

**One honest mismatch, and it should be written into the register rather than hidden.** Tikuisis 2004 found that caffeine restores **target engagement time but not accuracy or precision**, and Kamimori 2015 found **no caffeine effect on live-fire marksmanship** at all. But in this engine, aiming delay feeds `getAimDelayPenalty` inside `calculateHitChanceData`, so cutting aiming delay *does* improve the shot. The engine cannot separate the two halves the evidence separates. The mitigation is to keep the caffeine credit small (the 0.50 cap above) and to record that the mod grants caffeine an accuracy benefit the evidence explicitly denies — a limitation of the surface, not a finding.

### 5.5 Stress, panic, unhappiness and boredom coefficients

The stats are on different scales, so I use one convention throughout: **an effect of SMD 1.0 moves the stat by one moodle band.** A moodle band is the game's own unit of "the player notices", which makes it the least arbitrary bridge available between a standardised mean difference and a game number. Band widths taken from the health report: STRESS ≈ 0.25, PANIC ≈ 24 (the 6→30 step), UNHAPPINESS ≈ 22, BOREDOM ≈ 25. **The convention is a game choice and must be registered as one.**

| stat | driver | measured effect | coefficient (at the driver's maximum) | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PANIC** | sleep loss | anxiety **SMD +0.57 to +0.63** | **+14** at 24 h awake, scaled linearly with sleep debt, cap +20 | Palmer 2024 — the strongest mood result in the report |
| **PANIC** | caffeine, low dose (<400 mg) | **SMD 0.61** | **+15**, proportional to dose up to 400 mg | Liu 2024 |
| **PANIC** | caffeine, >400 mg | **SMD 2.86** | **+40 at 400 mg rising to +65**; keep it below the 65 moodle step unless the dose is extreme | Liu 2024 — direction and the 400 mg knee are solid, the magnitude is not; cap generously |
| **PANIC** | dehydration ≥1.5 % BML | tension/anxiety up, P = 0.029 | **+6** — one moodle step, no more | Ganio 2011; **no effect size published**, so this is the minimum visible number |
| **UNHAPPINESS** | sleep loss | positive affect **SMD −0.27 to −1.14** | **+25** at severe sleep loss, scaled from +6 at mild | Palmer 2024 |
| **UNHAPPINESS** | EPA depletion | **SMD −0.50** (EPA-pure repletion) | **+11** at clinical EPA depletion; **zero for DHA** | Liao 2019 — and this is the row that argues for tracking EPA and DHA separately |
| **UNHAPPINESS** | vitamin D depletion | **SMD −0.32 per 1000 IU/d** | **+7**, and **discounted by half to +4** | Ghaemi 2024, discounted because shorter trials showed stronger effects — a small-study-bias signature |
| **UNHAPPINESS** | severe energy deficit | apathy, irritability (qualitative) | **+15**, labelled a game choice | Kalm & Semba 2005 — **no effect size exists**; this number is invented and must say so |
| **STRESS** | B-vitamin depletion | **SMD 0.23** | **+0.06** at the clinical grade | Young 2019 — the only micronutrient stress term with a pooled estimate |
| **STRESS** | dehydration ≥1.5 % BML | total mood disturbance up | **+0.06** (one moodle step) | Armstrong 2012; Ganio 2011 |
| **STRESS** | alcohol **hangover** | significant association, unpooled | **+0.10**, applied the morning after, never during intoxication | Rothman 2026 |
| **UNHAPPINESS / PANIC** | exercise | depression **SMD −0.61**, anxiety **SMD −0.47** | **−13 unhappiness, −11 panic** credit, decaying over hours; favour moderate over intense | Munro 2026 — the largest evidence base here (79 551 participants), and it says lower intensity did more for anxiety |
| **BOREDOM** | — | — | **nothing. Leave the stat entirely alone** | No nutritional, hydration, sleep or substance evidence for boredom exists in anything reachable |

A note on the shape rather than the size: Palmer 2024's clearest structural finding is that sleep loss reliably **removes positive affect** and reliably **adds anxiety**, while its effect on negative affect and depressive symptoms "varied by sleep loss type". A mod with four separate mood stats can honour that instead of flattening it — drive unhappiness and panic from sleep debt, and do **not** drive a generalised misery term.

### 5.6 What the evidence does not support, and should be omitted

This list is as much of the deliverable as the coefficients. Each line names the study that closes it.

1. **Hunger → reaction time or attention.** Acute fasting: **g = 0.02, CrI [−0.05, 0.10]**, 222 effect sizes, 3484 participants, low heterogeneity (Bamberg & Moreau 2025). A well-estimated zero, not an underpowered null. The reaction penalty must hang off modelled blood glucose deep into starvation, and a character who has skipped meals gets nothing.
2. **Any nutrient → hearing range.** 0.7 dB over three years at best (Durga 2007); 0.17 percentage points over five years (Chen 2025). Omit the whole channel.
3. **Dehydration → vision.** No evidence on acuity or contrast sensitivity exists; the literature is intraocular pressure and tear films.
4. **Magnesium → anxiety.** Boyle 2017 pooled nothing, found effects in about half of each subgroup, and states **no validated stress measure was administered in any included study**.
5. **B12 → cognition or mood.** Explicit null across 16 RCTs and 6276 subjects (Markun 2021).
6. **Vitamin D → anxiety.** "No significant effects on anxiety symptoms" (Ghaemi 2024). Vitamin D may touch unhappiness; it must not touch panic.
7. **B vitamins → anxiety or panic.** **SMD 0.03, p = 0.71** (Young 2019). Stress only.
8. **Glucose → mood.** Explicitly unaffected at 0, 15, 25, 50 and 60 g (Sünram-Lea 2011). Glucose moves memory, which this game does not model.
9. **Iron → depression, memory, or psychomotor skill.** Null on all three (Falkingham 2010; Fiani 2025). Iron gets attention and anxiety, nothing else.
10. **Any nutrient → boredom.** Nothing exists.
11. **Omega-3 or lutein → the vision cone.** Lutein's outcome is daylight contrast over 16 weeks (Machida 2020); omega-3's cognitive SMDs are implausibly large at GRADE low (Shahinfar 2025). Use omega-3 for mood via EPA only.
12. **β-carotene → dark adaptation.** Indistinguishable from placebo where preformed retinol worked (Congdon 2000).
13. **Caffeine → shooting accuracy.** Explicitly not restored by caffeine after 22 h awake (Tikuisis 2004); no effect on live-fire marksmanship (Kamimori 2015). The engine forces this one anyway — register it as a known wrongness.
14. **Alcohol → mood during intoxication.** Only the **hangover** has evidence (Rothman 2026); the long-term anxiety literature is explicitly inconsistent (D'Aquino 2024).
15. **Any top-up benefit in a replete character.** Iron's effects **vanished when iron-deficient participants were excluded** (Fiani 2025). The § 4.4 ladder structure is right and the balanced-diet bonuses should stay small and labelled.
16. **Caffeine tolerance as a peak-reducing term.** Effects were unrelated to habitual consumption (Kløve & Petersen 2025).

## Proposed model

The shortest honest statement of what §§ 1–5 add up to, for the effects table the design's § 4.5 describes.

**One scale, not four.** Define the impairment unit as 24 h total sleep deprivation (§ 5.0) and express sleep debt, dehydration, hypoglycaemia, iron depletion, alcohol and caffeine on it. This is possible only because Falleti 2003 and Maruff 2005 measured sleep loss and alcohol *against each other*, which is the one cross-calibration in the whole literature, and it happens to be the pair the engine's cone formula also reads together. Everything else pegs to Lim & Dinges' g = −0.776.

**Perception through two gates and nothing else.** Vitamin A repletion, gated on zinc and slowed by iron or riboflavin depletion, grants and withdraws `NIGHT_VISION` with hysteresis and an ownership flag; clinical vitamin A deficiency alone toggles `SHORT_SIGHTED` behind the severity dial. The cone otherwise moves only because the mod already owns fatigue and intoxication, and **hearing gets no nutrient term at all**. This is a smaller perception surface than the design's § 4.5 implies, and the reason is § 2: the nutritional hearing literature tops out at 0.7 dB over three years.

**Reaction through aiming delay, driven by blood glucose and not by hunger.** `aimingDelayMult = clamp(0.90, 1.60, 1 + 0.25 × IU_total)`, re-applied after each shot because `CombatManager.setAimingDelay` overwrites it. The single most important line in this report is that acute fasting does nothing measurable (g = 0.02 over 3484 participants), so the term must read the model's glucose state at 3.0–3.5 mmol/L, not the hunger bar. Caffeine's credit is multiplicative on the sleep-debt penalty rather than additive, because its size is conditional on being impaired.

**Mood through one convention: SMD 1.0 = one moodle band.** Sleep debt drives panic (+14 at 24 h) and unhappiness (+25 at severe loss) and nothing else; EPA depletion and vitamin D depletion touch unhappiness only; B vitamins touch stress only; caffeine charges panic with a knee at 400 mg; alcohol charges stress the morning after, never during; exercise credits both. Boredom is untouched.

**The omission list is a deliverable.** Sixteen mechanics in § 5.6 are named as unsupported with the study that closes each. Three of them — hunger-to-reaction-time, nutrient-to-hearing, magnesium-to-anxiety — are mechanics a designer would reach for by instinct and that the evidence actively refutes rather than merely failing to support.

**Two asymmetries worth preserving.** Sleep loss removes positive affect and adds anxiety but does not uniformly worsen negative affect (Palmer 2024) — a four-stat mood model can represent that instead of flattening it. And sleep deprivation degrades both target detection and marksmanship while caffeine restores only detection (Tikuisis 2004) — which the engine cannot represent, and that failure should be written down rather than papered over.

## Gaps

**Searches that could not be run.** The session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before this report began, so discovery ran entirely through the Europe PMC REST search API. That API's free-text relevance ranking is strongly recency-biased and its `sort=CITED` parameter is **not query-scoped** (a hypoglycaemia-and-cognition query sorted by citations returned AHA statistics reports), so all productive searching used `TITLE:` and `AUTH:` field queries. The consequence is a systematic bias toward papers whose *titles* contain the search terms. Specifically not found, and possibly existing:

- **A meta-analysis of sleep deprivation on *auditory* vigilance.** Everything found was visual (the PVT and relatives). The § 5.1 conclusion that hearing follows fatigue rests on the engine's wiring, not on auditory-specific evidence.
- **McLellan, Caldwell & Lieberman 2016**, the caffeine review the brief named. `AUTH:"McLellan TM"` returned sixteen of his papers but not that review. Kløve & Petersen 2025 and Irwin 2020 are used in its place and are both more recent and meta-analytic, so the substitution is an upgrade, but the named paper was not verified.
- **Pross 2014** on hydration and mood. `AUTH:"Pross N"` returned only a hydration-biomarker paper (Perrier 2013). Armstrong 2012 and Ganio 2011 cover the same ground with RCT evidence in both sexes.
- **A pooled effect size for dehydration on the *fatigue* construct specifically.** Armstrong 2012 and Ganio 2011 report p-values, not effect sizes, for fatigue-inertia and tension. The § 5.1 fatigue offsets (+0.06 to +0.10) therefore have **no magnitude evidence at all** and are pure game choices.
- **Any measured interaction between two impairment drivers.** Nothing found measures sleep loss × dehydration, sleep loss × alcohol, or dehydration × hypoglycaemia together. § 5.4's additivity is an assumption and is flagged as one in the combination rules.

**Numbers used that rest on derivation rather than publication.**

- The **dehydration attention-by-threshold rescaling** in § 5.0 (0.45 IU below 2 % BML, 0.89 above) multiplies Wittbrodt's published attention-domain effect by the ratio of her published threshold subgroups. That interaction was not published and the arithmetic is mine.
- The **BAC-to-`INTOXICATION` mapping** (0.05 % ≈ `I = 25`) is an inference from the Drunk moodle thresholds. Nothing in the jar states what BAC `INTOXICATION = 100` represents, and no study maps either onto the other.
- The **SMD 1.0 = one moodle band** convention in § 5.5 is a bridge I invented. It is defensible because a moodle band is the game's own noticeability unit, but it is not evidence.
- The **0.25 coefficient** in the aiming-delay formula is a free parameter. The evidence fixes the ratios between its terms and says nothing about its scale.

**Evidence-quality caveats carried forward.**

- **Keys 1950 was not read.** Everything about the Minnesota experiment comes from Kalm & Semba's verified 2005 account, which supplies a symptom list and **not one effect size**. Every semi-starvation mood number in § 5.5 is therefore invented, and the register rows must say so.
- **Hypoglycaemia's magnitude comes from type 2 diabetes patients**, not healthy adults (Nilsson 2019). The threshold (Warren & Frier 2005) is from healthy subjects; the −0.7 SD is not.
- **Iron's hearing cohort is female-only** (Chen 2025), as is the serum-iron study (Jung 2025) and the dietary-nutrient cohort (Curhan 2015). No male data was found for any of it.
- **Falkingham 2010 found no RCTs in men or older people**, so the iron-attention SMD of 0.59 — one of the load-bearing numbers in § 5.4 — rests entirely on children, adolescents and women.
- **The vitamin A repletion evidence is almost all pregnant Nepali women** (Haskell 2005; Congdon 2000; Graham 2007). The one adult general-population study is 20 liver-transplant candidates (Abbott-Johnson 2011). Both the timescales and the thresholds in § 5.2 extrapolate across a large population gap.
- **Zinc's night-blindness evidence is six cirrhotics and one case report.** It is used only as a gate, never as a ladder, for exactly this reason.
- **Two meta-analyses report magnitudes I would not carry at face value**: Shahinfar 2025 (omega-3 attention SMD 0.98 at GRADE low) and Liu 2024 (caffeine anxiety SMD 2.86 at high dose, 14 studies, 546 participants). Both are used for shape and direction only, and § 5.5 caps the caffeine panic term well below what its point estimate implies.
- **Ghaemi 2024's duration gradient runs the wrong way** — shorter trials showed stronger effects — which is the classic small-study-bias signature. The vitamin D unhappiness coefficient is halved for this reason and the discount is stated in the table.
- **One fetched abstract contained an internal inconsistency**: Irwin 2020's attention-response-time confidence interval prints as "0.53–0.83" against a point estimate of g = 0.86. That CI is not used anywhere.
- **Three sources are systematic reviews without pooling** and are labelled MA in the grade column for want of a finer grade: Boyle 2017, Rothman 2026 and D'Aquino 2024. None supplies an effect size, and § 5.6 omits the mechanic Boyle 2017 is usually cited for.
- **Two marksmanship papers have no DOI** in their Europe PMC records (Tikuisis 2004, PMID 15460628; McLellan 2005, PMID 15672985). Both were verified by PMID with title, authors, year and journal matched.

**What a live run would settle that this report cannot.** Whether the night-vision grant is perceptible at all through the cone's `lerp` at rate 0.033 and its `private static` `visionConeLerp` (which the jar report notes is one value per *process*, not per character); whether a server-side trait write plus `sendSyncPlayerFields(player, 2)` actually reaches the client, which the design already lists as an experiment; and whether a fatigue offset of +0.06 is visible to a player at all, which decides whether § 5.1's hydration term is worth its coupling to the sleep system.
