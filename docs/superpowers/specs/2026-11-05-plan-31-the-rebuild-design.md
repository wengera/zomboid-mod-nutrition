# Plan 31 — the rebuild (design)

Status: draft, 2026-11-05. Supersedes the small-fix loop of Plans 24 to 30 as the method; the agent base, the harness and the evidence rules stay.

## 0. Angus's direction

Three directions of 2026-11-05 set this plan (the memories `stand-and-fight-dress-first`, `rebuild-not-tweak`, `fast-exploratory-trials`):

1. Far too much running away and aimless running, little combat, no wound-dressing priority. One zombie is never a reason to flee: stand and kill it. Dressing comes first, even by tearing own clothing. Swings and stomps aim at the head. The stance faces the zombie.
2. A thorough rebuild of how the player is controlled, with major changes compared and contrasted, options thought out by subagents on controlling the player like a real player; not minor rule changes and retests.
3. Tighter iterations and faster tests: many quick, bold exploratory trials that try things out, break things and discover, then a clean-up and hardening of what worked.

The standing directions hold: the two metrics are kills and hits per kill (`kills-metric`, `hits-per-kill-metric`); every wound is analyzed for its cause and tuned out while the character keeps approaching, never by fleeing more (`combat-approach-not-flee`); no sprinting; clear then loot, never wander; a lot more sneaking and intent at doors (`intent-doors-sneak`); a timing-critical physical action is a Lua reflex under a Python maneuver (`cerebellum-layer`).

## 1. What the readings say

**F15** (`testing/artifacts/f_goals_16-20261105-164332/f_grade.txt`, the first session on iteration 24): no kill, 8 landed attacks, a death at game minute 702. 27 fights, every one fled; of the 298 flee lines, 211 had one standing zombie or none within 6 tiles. 26 melees sent, 19 ended `failed superseded` by the next tick's rule; 16 of 20 model goals ended `superseded`; all 9 unwears for a rag were refused `moving`. The wound icon stood for 115 game minutes; one bandage was done, on the run. The character bled out running and exploring from one slow zombie at 3 to 6 tiles with health 2.11 (`visual-review-f15.md`, shots 184 to 195). The visual pass agrees with the frames on the facing: at every press with a zombie in frame the character was squared on it (shots 15, 16, 93, 94). What is wrong is around the press.

**The swing that lands nothing.** Under P30-A the 22 strike swings were pressed at 0.95 to 0.98 tiles while the chaser closed at about 1.6 tiles a second; the damage is applied at the swing's collision event, 25 % into a clip of 1.0 s / CombatSpeed (#4665, #4668), by which time the zombie read 0.31 to 0.55 tiles away, inside the bat's `MinRange` 0.61 (the item script; the mechanism is J2 below). None of the 22 took more than 0.013 off a zombie. E21's swings at 1.058 to 1.385 tiles all lowered and its swings under 0.923 none (#4596); F13's at 1.147 to 1.444 all lowered (#4631). Every bite of F14 and E23 landed in the reflex's back step after a press that did nothing (#4639, #4649). A second reading of the same swings (`f15-read.md`): the first fight's bites wounded both hands, the arms' pain then read 65 to 153, and pain over 10 divides every swing that is not a stomp by about the pain over 10 (#4370), so the six swings that landed took 0.0055 to 0.0125 off zombies at health 2.1 to 2.3, while the reflex stayed in strike mode (P28-H) though the stomp is not divided; the two knock-downs were never finished because a `pair` flee broke each finish. The two readings are not rivals: the pain made every swing futile, the press distance made most of them land nothing, and the undressed hands drove the `pair` and `held` flee clauses through the wound count. One undressed wound at game minute 587 decided the session. (The F15 numbers are desk reads of the state log, adjudicated in F15's write-up before they are rows.)

**The common diagnosis.** Four independent design reads (`.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-05-plan-30-the-intent/rebuild-proposal-*.md`) converge: the 2 Hz re-choice that re-runs every branch each tick and supersedes the running action is the structure that produces the flee ladder, its eight latches, the holds and the yields, and it cuts the reflex off before its swing. A real player holds one intent, fights what can be fought, dresses before moving on and walks somewhere for a reason. The swing's landing is a reflex problem that no decider fixes.

## 2. The architecture every variant shares

Three layers, one owner each:

| Layer | Rate | Owns | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| The decider | 2 Hz, plus interrupts | which intent or play, and its target | **the variant** (§ 3), forked at the top of `Survive.assess` by `Survive(control=...)` |
| The plays | 2 Hz Python over a Lua reflex at the client's tick | how: the stance, the facing, the press, the shove, the stomp, the step, the dressing, the walk | shared executors (§ 4) |
| The model | per goal, 4 to 14 s | where to go and what to loot | `goals.py`, `llm.py`, kept strategic (the tactician variant tests the alternative) |

The ladder stays whole behind the switch as the control arm. With the switch off, the six goldens stay byte-identical.

## 3. The variants

Three deciders over the same plays, and the control:

- **D, the doctrine** (the intent-machine and combat-doctrine proposals, one architecture). An arbiter holds exactly one intent, chosen by hard priorities in order: a zombie in reach is struck; a bleeding part is dressed; three or more aware zombies are avoided toward a named refuge; one or two aware zombies are fought in the stance to the kill; a door with a zombie behind it is decided once; else the standing order (loot, travel, stock, the needs). An intent ends `done`, `failed` by its own test, or `preempted` by a strictly higher one; nothing of equal or lower priority interrupts; a pre-empted intent waits in a one-deep resume slot. Health is not a flee reason. Package `planner/doctrine/` (`arbiter.py`, `intents.py`, the constants with their sources).
- **U, the utility** (the situation-utility proposal). About once a second and on five interrupts, six to ten candidate acts (fight z, dress p, travel, sneak, retreat to a filter, loot, rest) are scored by expected kill value, expected hits, the bleed over the span and the goal's progress; the held act is replaced only by a margin and after a minimum span; a lone chaser always scores fight; there is no flee act, only a retreat to a filter that ends in the stance. The weights are bounded by a linear program over decision lines labelled from Angus's directions, and every line logs each candidate's terms. Package `planner/utility/`.
- **T, the tactician** (the model-as-tactician proposal). The model is asked on every situation change (a notice, a count change, a hit, a bleed, a kill, a play's end, an arrival), never on a clock, for one play from a closed vocabulary with a target label and a bound; the current play runs on while the call is out; a fallback table of Angus's rules answers at zero latency when a bound breaks; a late answer is validated against the moment's view. Its offline bench (a cached table against the model over 200 recorded trigger views) runs before any boot; if the table ties the model, T is the table. Package `planner/tactician/`.
- **L, the control.** The ladder at HEAD with the shared Phase 0 reflex change, so the comparison isolates the decider.

Each variant drops, on its own path, the flee ladder and its hysteresis, `hold.py`, `care_yield.py`, `pull.py`, `engage.py`, `still.py` and the Fight's per-tick ladder, and keeps the World, the protocol, the Lua skills, the edge routing, the exits, the water chain and the goal source. The composition step of § 5 may mix them.

## 4. Phase 0: what every variant needs first

1. **The meet (the reflex).** The facing is written to the target from about 3 tiles and the press waits until the body's real angle is on the target (J5: a 90 degree turn takes about 0.4 s while `dir` reads the new facing at once; a new frame field carries the body's angle). The swing is pressed when the chaser's predicted distance at the collision event, d minus the closing speed times the time to collision, falls inside the reach band and the angle holds (J2: `MinRange` is not checked at the collision, so a zombie that closes in during the swing still takes the hit; the press itself must be made beyond 0.61 or it becomes a shove). After the press there is no back step and no pause: the character keeps pressing while squared on the zombie, since a player mid-attack facing an attacking zombie within 45 degrees is never bitten (J4) and a landed hit staggers or stuns it (J3); knocked down → the finish; lowered → press again as the swing ends; did nothing → press again. Every press is logged `{d_press, d_collide_pred, body_angle_off, hp0, hp1, knock, cut_by}` so each wound traces to the press before it. The constants come from J2 to J5, not from a fit to the frames. This is a PZA/1.10 change to `PZAgent_StanceMelee.lua` (or a `play` command carrying `meet`, `finish`, `string`, `filter_door`), with its schema, `bus_inventory`, `luabalance` and offline tests.
2. **The finish.** The floor swing within reach, else the stomp under 0.3 tiles, with the facing pointed at the fallen zombie's head (J1: the floor attack tests the weapon's line along the facing against the head first, ×3; a stomp reaches 0.3), repeated until `zombie_dead`, with no time end; a standing zombie inside 2 tiles hands back to the meet. The frame carries the fallen zombie's head position or its prone direction. A finish is never broken by a second zombie beyond 2 tiles (F15's two knock-downs were lost to `pair` flees).
2b. **Spent.** With the arms' pain over 10 or the endurance or tired moodle at 2, the meet becomes shove and stomp (the stomp is not divided by the arms' pain, #4370; a shove's critical knocks down, #4373), no new fight is sought, and the character rests once clear.
3. **The dress (a maneuver).** The character stops (the nine `moving` refusals), dresses the worst bleeding part with a held bandage or rag, and with none takes off the outer garment, rips it and bandages with the rag; an unwashed rag is acceptable while bleeding; an upper-body dressing may ride a walk. One commitment, not three round trips (a composite Lua `dress` skill if the round trips prove slow).
4. **The fork.** `Survive(control="ladder"|"doctrine"|"utility"|"tactician")` at the top of `assess`, the ladder the default, the goldens unchanged.
5. **The trial loop** (§ 5's instrument; the scoping read `trial-harness-scoping.md` in Plan 30's workspace):
   - **The arena fixture `arena-east`**, made once in one boot: the `agent` character is moved by the game's own admin command `teleportto` over RCON (`xs.rcon`) to about 15 tiles from the cluster F15 found near (10764, 10618), the session torn down and the world saved as a fixture by `pzt snapshot --from-run`. **The street fixture `street-1`** is made the same way beside a row of unlooted houses. Position, the `Zombies` density and the start hour belong to a fixture; spawns, noise and the kit are per trial.
   - **Waves per trial**: the game's RCON command `createhorde2` spawns a horde at an exact square; the driver computes the square from the character's position plus a distance and a bearing, under named wave plans. The RCON forms of both commands are read off the jar and confirmed by the first smoke boot.
   - **The generic driver `x_trial.py --variant <name> --fixture <f> [--minutes N] [--no-llm] [--waves <plan>]`** over `driverlib/trial.py`: `day.boot_and_run` without the auto-copy into `testing/artifacts/` and without the chain's next steps; it writes `trial.json` (the variant, the switches, the commit or the snapshot stamp, the dirty flag and the diff hash) and refuses unknown arguments; the `claude -p` probe runs only when the model is used; the pre-boot checks run offline against a real run layout.
   - **The snapshot boot**: `tools/trial_snapshot.py` exports a commit (or the working tree with its diff) by `git archive` into a scratch root, one tree per sha, writes `TRIAL_STAMP.json` and prints the launch line; `testing/pzt/paths.py` reads `PZT_FIXTURES` and `PZT_RUNS` from the environment so the snapshot uses the main tree's fixture caches and run folder. The session finds its code relative to its own files and the game reads the mod Lua from the copy made at boot, so the main tree may change while a trial runs. No full pytest runs beside a trial (0.73 GB free at ready).
   - **The scorecard `tools/trial_score.py <run> [--seen "..."]`** imports the live readers (`grades/f12.py`'s union hits, kills matched, goal wait, standing; the melee reader) and adds four readings (the minutes fleeing or retreating, the minutes toward a named target, the wounds dressed within one game minute, the melees superseded before their swing), prints the row and appends it to `testing/trials/trials.md` (tracked; `--rescore-all` rebuilds the table from the run folders).
   - **The length**: a five-minute loop is about 7.3 minutes of wall time with the boot and the teardown, inside one foreground call under the ten-minute cap, so a trial needs no detached launch; about six trials an hour, bound by the boot and the one-session rule, not memory. One trial per launch: a second session would inherit the first's wounds and position.
   - **The fork and the lanes**: `Survive(control=...)` dispatches in `assess` to the old body verbatim as the default, variant packages load by name so no shared registry is edited, the Lua reflex and the schema are frozen for Explore once Phase 0's reflex change lands, each variant is built in its own worktree and a trial may boot from a branch head that never reaches `main`. ## 5. Explore: the trials

The method is many short live trials, scored by script, with no evidence ceremony per trial.

- **A trial** is one boot of the generic driver with a variant, a fixture and a wall-time cap (five to ten minutes), on a snapshot of a named commit. The run folder stays per machine. The scorecard appends one row to `testing/trials/trials.md` (tracked): the variant, its commit, kills by the events, hits by the union, hits per kill, the minutes fleeing or retreating, the minutes moving toward a named target, the wounds dressed within one game minute, the melees superseded before their swing, the end and the health at the end, plus three lines of what was seen. No register row, no write-up, no artifact commit, no visual reviewer, no frozen grade.
- **The fixtures.** An arena that puts the character into a fight within the first game minute and keeps them coming (for the combat trials, with the scripted goal source and no model call), and a street of unlooted houses with zombies between them (for the movement, door, sneak and dressing trials, with the model).
- **The matrix.** Each of D, U, T and L runs two arena trials and one street trial; T's offline bench runs first and may replace T by its table. Twelve trials is the first round. A trial that breaks the character is a result and keeps its row.
- **The composition.** After the round, one read across the trials log names what worked in each variant (a decider, a play, a constant, a view) and composes one or two mixed builds behind their own switches; a second round of trials compares them with the round's best. A row whose variant differs from the last by a constant alone is the pattern Angus has ruled out, and the trials log makes it visible.
- **What binds every boot.** `pzt doctor`, the 10 GB floor read by `xlib.free_gb` and ledgered, one live session at a time, Angus's own client never touched, no OS cursor, the detached launch with a shown or minimized console polled in foreground calls under ten minutes, no sprint, and the wound analysis: every wound in a trial is traced to the press or leg before it in the trial's three lines.

## 6. The jar reads before the rules

Read on 42.21 (`jar-read-swing.md` in Plan 30's workspace, 14 candidate claims, minted as rows on `docs/platform/agent.md` after review; each statement below is that read's, grade C, and the rows carry the lines):

- **J1, the head.** On an upright zombie a bat hit strikes a body part rolled uniformly over the ten upper parts, the head one in ten, and the part only picks which worn clothing defends; the player cannot choose it. On a fallen zombie a floor attack or stomp tests the weapon's line along the attacker's forward direction against the zombie's bones, head first (the head ×3 damage, the spine ×1, a leg or foot ×0.05, no bone nothing), the bat's reach 1.0 tile and a stomp's 0.3. So "aim for the head" is real only on the floor, and it means pointing the facing at the fallen zombie's head: the finish needs the fallen zombie's head position (or its prone direction) in the frame.
- **J2, the re-check.** At the collision event the hit list is rebuilt: a target drops out on posture (an upright swing drops every prone zombie), distance beyond 3 tiles, height, the angle against `MinAngle` (cosine 0.75), reach beyond `MaxRange` (plus 0.2 against a lunging zombie), a blocked line or a closed door. **`MinRange` is never checked at the collision**: a zombie that closes inside 0.61 during the swing still takes the full hit; `MinRange` turns the press into a shove only at the press or the swing's start. So F15's unchanged swings were not lost to the range (§ 1's first reading is withdrawn as a mechanism; the write-up reads each swing's angle, hit reaction and pain).
- **J3, the knock-down and the stagger.** A critical swing of any weapon knocks the zombie down (an uncharged press rolls about 32 + 3 × Blunt for the bat; `KnockdownMod` is read by no combat code). A landed swing on a zombie attacking or lunging within 0.9 tiles staggers it; any other hit puts it in a hit reaction of about 1.8 s; either cancels its attack.
- **J4, the zombie's attack.** The bite starts at 0.72 tiles while facing the target, the lunge from 3.5 tiles; the bite's damage check comes about 0.51 s after the attack starts (the live readings say about 0.70 s; the gap is unexplained) and needs a Manhattan distance of at most 1.0 and no wall, window or door between. **A player mid-attack who faces the zombie within about 45 degrees and is not aiming at the floor is never bitten.** So the swing is the defence: a character that keeps pressing while squared on an attacking zombie takes no bite, and the back step is the worst answer.
- **J5, the turn.** The body turns toward a written facing at about 206 degrees a second walking (165 running), the upper-body twist faster but limited to 15 degrees (45 aiming); standing still and not attacking it only twists unless the facing is more than a degree beyond the twist limit; the collision reads the angle the body has reached, so a swing pressed mid-turn swings at the partly turned angle; a 90 degree turn takes about 0.4 s while the frames' `dir` reads the new facing at once. This is the mismatch Angus sees: the meet faces early, from about 3 tiles, and presses only once the body's real angle is on the target (a frame field for the body's actual angle).
- **J6, windows and fences.** A climbing zombie has no way into its attack and cannot bite across a wall, window or door; a hit after its climb has started knocks it down on the far side; a swing smashes a closed window between and still lands. A fence or a window is a filter that drops zombies one at a time.

Still unread: whether a low fence counts as a wall between squares; the bat clips' own rotation; the zombie-armour sandbox defaults; how a stagger set on the attacking client reaches the zombie's owner in multiplayer.

## 7. Harden

The composition that scored best over two rounds gets the full treatment once: its tests, a grade module, two evidence sessions with artifacts under § 5 of CLAUDE.md, the visual pass, the write-up with its rows, and the close. If it beats the ladder on the trials and the evidence sessions, the ladder's path and its pinned tests go, deleted by name in the task report. If no variant beats the ladder, the report says so and the ladder stays.

## 8. Measures and falsifiers

Per trial and per evidence session: kills; hits per kill both ways (the frames' reaction runs, and their union with the reflex's `hit` steps); the minutes fleeing or retreating, and the minutes moving away from a chaser with no filter target (must read near zero under D and U); the minutes toward a named target and the goals' `done` share; the wounds dressed within one game minute; the melees superseded before their swing (F15: 19 of 26; must fall to near zero); equal-priority switches under D (must read zero); the presses by predicted collision distance against their outcome (the meet's own test: presses inside the band lower their target as E21's and F13's did, with no bite in a back step, since there is none). A gap inside an arm's own spread is no gap: F14 and E23 on one iteration gave 7 kills and 1.

## 9. Risks

- **Commitment amplifies a bad swing.** Every decider fights every lone zombie to the end; if the meet's timing does not make the swing land, hits per kill rise. Phase 0 lands the meet first and the first arena trials read it on L before any decider.
- **A fixed book is brittle off its pages**: crawlers, an indoor corner, zombies arriving one after another. The classes stay coarse and a new play is added only from a wound analysis.
- **"Never flee one" can kill a character** at low health with an undressed bleed. Accepted on Angus's direction; the risk is routed to the dressing's priority.
- **The utility's weights rest on six PZA/1.9 hits**, and a closed-loop oscillation shows only live.
- **The tactician's edge is the ambiguous middle, where its latency costs most**; its offline bench decides before a boot.
- **Twelve trials on one machine**: at ten minutes each plus the boot and the teardown, a round is a working day of sessions. The scoping read bounds it.
