# The science register

The science register `docs/reference/science.tsv` holds every evidenced value the nutrition mod may ship: a requirement, a store size, an onset time, an effect size, a threshold, a rate, a fitted model constant. It is the second of the two registers the mod cites (spec § 0): the claims register says what the game does, the science register says what the evidence says, and a number in the mod with no row in either does not ship.

## Columns

| column | what it holds |
|---|---|
| `id` | `S` and four digits, contiguous from `S0001`; the mod cites the bare id (`S0000` is this page's illustration and is never minted) |
| `topic` | one slug of the controlled vocabulary below, so the rows a nutrient record or a system model needs are one filter away; empty on an open row |
| `parameter` | the quantity, as a noun phrase, with the direction of an effect where the row is an effect |
| `value` | the number and its unit, or the qualitative finding |
| `range` | the uncertainty or spread as the source gives it — a confidence interval, a study count, a between-study range |
| `population` | who was studied and in what context; every value is a central tendency for that population |
| `grade` | `MA`, `RCT`, `COH`, `EXP`, `MODEL`, `AUTH` or `TXT` (below); empty on an open row, because nothing was graded |
| `citation` | authors, year, journal, then the record: `doi:10.…`, `pmid:…`, `url:https://…` for an authority page, `isbn:…` for a book; two records separated by `; ` |
| `source` | the research report and section the row was harvested from, readable at the tag `design-phase-v1` |
| `status` | `settled`, `open` (a named gap: topic, grade, value and citation may be empty), `unverified` (a number quoted but not resolved), `superseded` |
| `successor` | the row that replaces a superseded one |

## Topics

Nutrients: `vitamin-a`, `vitamin-d`, `vitamin-e`, `vitamin-k`, `vitamin-c`, `thiamine`, `riboflavin`, `niacin`, `pantothenate`, `vitamin-b6`, `biotin`, `folate`, `vitamin-b12`, `choline`, `sodium`, `potassium`, `chloride`, `calcium`, `magnesium`, `phosphorus`, `iron`, `zinc`, `copper`, `iodine`, `selenium`, `manganese`, `water`, `fibre`, `essential-fats`, `carbohydrate-quality`, `alcohol`, `caffeine`, `phytate`, `sweat`, `digestion`. Systems: `energy`, `body-composition`, `lean-mass`, `strength`, `aerobic-capacity`, `glycogen`, `protein`, `fatigue-sleep`, `cognition`, `mood`, `perception`, `healing`, `immunity`, `thermal`, `toxicity`, `starvation`, `refeeding`, `satiety` (meal-level satiation, satiety and time-to-hunger rows). `general` is for a cross-cutting row that fits no other; a nutrient's upper limit or toxic dose takes the nutrient's slug, and `toxicity` holds poisoning rows that belong to no single nutrient. A row on a two-nutrient interaction takes the slug of the nutrient whose absorption or status is the measured quantity, and a sweat concentration sits under `sweat` (the Baker table, S0518), not under the ion. The vocabulary is `sciencelib.TOPICS`; a slug is requested in a harvest task's report and added by the controller in the commit that lands the rows, with the reason.

## Grades

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| `MA` | a meta-analysis, network meta-analysis, meta-regression or systematic review |
| `RCT` | a randomised controlled trial, a controlled crossover or an inpatient controlled trial |
| `EXP` | a controlled human depletion or dosing experiment that is not randomised |
| `COH` | a cohort, an observational or cross-sectional study, a regression derived from one, or a case report |
| `AUTH` | an authority report — a DRI, an EFSA opinion, a position stand, a consensus statement |
| `TXT` | a narrative review, a textbook chapter, an editorial or a modelling paper |
| `MODEL` | a published mathematical model cited for its shape and fitted constants, never for an effect size |

A row's grade is the source's, never the row's importance. A source graded with a slashed pair takes the left-most member of the rank `MA`, `RCT`, `EXP`, `COH`, `AUTH`, `TXT`, whichever order it is written in; `MODEL` never appears in a pair, because a model cited beside a trial is two rows. Where a meta-analysis and a later update disagree, the row carries the later reading and both records. Two irreconcilable sources are two `settled` rows whose `parameter` names the disagreement; the design's choice between them is a ruling, not a row.

## Rules

- A value the mod ships rests on a `settled` row; an `open` or `unverified` row names what is missing and ships nothing.
- Every `settled` row's citation was resolved against its record through Europe PMC or Crossref before the row was minted: title, first author and year matched.
- A game choice — a saturation constant, a clamp, a cadence, a dial's default — is not a row; the row is the cited value the choice rests on, and the choice is named as one where it is made.
- An `open` row's constant ships as a labelled game choice, never as a cited value, when the literature gives no basis for it: the insect dry-matter fraction of 0.30 (S1188) is the case, its direction read from the papers named in the row and its size the plan's.
- `python tools/science_check.py` checks the register's schema before every commit that touches it; `python tools/science_check.py --scan <the mod's tree>` resolves every `S` id token the mod carries and fails a token that names no row or a superseded one; the scan is never given `tools/` or `docs/`, which carry the reserved illustration in prose.
- A row is minted by the controller from a task's part file with `python tools/science_delta.py apply <part.tsv>`; a writer never edits the register.
- An `open` row closes by `python tools/science_delta.py settle <id> …`, which fills its cells and settles it, or by `status <id> superseded --successor <id>`, which leaves its value, grade and citation cells empty (it may keep its topic slug) and points at the row that answers it.
- A superseded row keeps its id and names its successor; a row withdrawn after minting goes `unverified` or `superseded`, never deleted; contiguity is a minting invariant, not a repair path.
- A row is self-sufficient: its citation is the record, and its source is provenance.

## Sources

The rows were distilled from the eight science reports of the design phase (2026-09-27), each of which resolved every citation it carried through Europe PMC and matched title, authors, journal and year before quoting a number. One paragraph per report follows, in the order the reports were distilled; the row ranges are the ids each report's harvest minted.

**Energy and body** (`science-energy-body.md`, rows S0001–S0132). The report supplies resting expenditure (the Mifflin–St Jeor equations and their fat-free-mass form, organ-specific rates, adaptive thermogenesis), activity expenditure from the 2024 Adult Compendium with the Pandolf load-carriage terms and shivering, body-composition dynamics (Alpert's fat-store ceiling, the Forbes and Hall partitioning relations, the energy density of weight lost), protein and muscle, muscle memory, the strength-to-lean-mass relation, hydration, the starvation and refeeding timeline, and the effects of excess adiposity. Its evidence is mostly meta-analyses, controlled trials and authority reports, with model papers graded as narrative sources, and every citation was re-resolved at harvest. Almost every value comes from healthy young-to-middle-aged adults, athletes, obese clinic patients or critically ill inpatients, none from people doing weeks of manual labour under food scarcity, so each value is a central tendency to be widened rather than a constant. Two pairs of sources stand as paired rows — the dehydration level that impairs endurance, and refeeding-syndrome incidence — and the dehydration-and-strength reading is Savoie 2015 (strength −5.5 %, muscular endurance −8.3 %), replacing the earlier review's "no clear effect". The largest gaps, each an open or unverified row, are the quantities a body model needs most: the energy densities of fat and lean tissue, total glycogen in grams, the gastric-emptying constants, the primary Pandolf coefficients and terrain factors, and any evidenced time to death without food or water; two open rows filed at Plan 3 name the adaptive-thermogenesis magnitude under a sustained deficit and the Forbes and Hall partition coefficient, each with its direction settled and its size not.

**Vitamins** (`science-vitamins.md`, rows S0133–S0388). The report gives the requirements, upper levels, store sizes and half-lives, deficiency and toxicity thresholds and sign sequences for vitamins A, D, E and K, vitamin C, the eight B vitamins and choline, mostly from the IOM reports confirmed through the Linus Pauling Institute pages, the Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023 scoping reviews and EFSA's opinions, with the cooking, canning and processing losses consolidated per vitamin. Its cross-cutting section adds the meal-level couplings a food model needs — fat gating carotenoid and vitamin D absorption, vitamin C and phytate on non-haem iron (both as multipliers on the natural log of absorption, not percentage points) — and the depletion clocks the water-soluble vitamins carry (scurvy at about 40 days of nil intake, thiamine within weeks, folate and B12 over months to years). Every value is a central tendency for the population studied: healthy, sedentary, temperate-climate adults for the requirements, deficient children and pregnant women for the vitamin A supplementation effects, and older women for the fracture pooling, so applying them to a labouring, injured adult is an extrapolation. The vitamin D respiratory-infection effect and the vitamin C colds effect are minted with the immunity rows (S0946 on), not here, and two authority requirements a factor of two apart (vitamin B12, EFSA against the Nordic recommendation) stand as two rows. The largest gap is timing: no measured half-life of the hepatic vitamin A store, no dietary-depletion clock for vitamin E, riboflavin or pantothenate, no fetched timings from Ferland's vitamin K restriction, and no cooking retention for vitamins D, E or K.

**Minerals, fibre and fats** (`science-minerals-fibre-fats.md`, rows S0389–S0572). The report gives the IOM and NASEM requirement, upper-limit and body-store rows for the twelve minerals and electrolytes with the sweat concentrations of sodium, potassium and chloride, the deficiency and depletion findings for each (magnesium's urinary conservation, Prasad's seven-week zinc depletion, iron's depletion sequence and work-capacity meta-analyses, selenosis and Keshan disease), the fibre, essential-fatty-acid, carbohydrate-quality, very-low-carbohydrate, sweat, alcohol and caffeine rows, and a cross-cutting set of absorption and loss interactions (phytate on iron and magnesium, vitamin C and whole-diet iron bioavailability, zinc on copper, magnesium on potassium, iron and selenium on thyroid function, alcohol and caffeine on urinary minerals). Every settled row was verified against a Europe PMC or Crossref record, and the report's own unverified figures are open rows rather than numbers. The requirement rows are North American adult reference values, while the kinetic and interaction rows come from small single-meal isotope studies and depletion experiments, mostly in healthy young men, and the sweat rows from exercising athletes, so a reading for a woman, an older adult or a sedentary person is an extrapolation. The single-meal absorption effects shrink in whole-diet studies, which the register carries as their own rows. The largest gap is time to onset on a fixed deficient intake, measured only for essential fatty acids, zinc and magnesium's urinary conservation, with repletion time constants missing for sodium, magnesium, potassium and iron.

**Strength training** (`wave2-science-strength-training.md`, rows S0573–S0660). The report covers the resistance-training dose-response for strength and size, protein and energy as the anabolic substrate and the deficit-to-strength relation, detraining and retraining time courses, the acute states that move maximal strength (hypohydration, sleep loss, caffeine, carbohydrate, alcohol), the micronutrients with a strength effect, and the relation of strength to lean mass and body weight. Its strongest rows are meta-analyses: load drives strength and sets drive size with diminishing returns, total protein plateaus at 1.62 g/kg/day, strength loss tracks cumulative energy deficit and recovers within a week in one trial, strength falls two to four times faster than muscle in disuse, and hypohydration and sleep loss cost a few to several percent. Almost every number is a central tendency from six-to-twenty-week supervised gym trials in healthy young, mostly male, adults, with some older-adult and military data, and the between-person spread (1RM gains of 0 to 250 % in one programme) is larger than any mean. Two disagreements stand as paired rows: hypohydration costs about 2 % (narrative) or 5.5 % (meta-analysis) of strength, and vitamin D helps young adults by SMD 0.03 or 0.32 depending on the synthesis. The largest gap is the absence of any study of strength under combined underfeeding, sleep loss, dehydration and heavy work, followed by the missing per-week detraining curve, the lean-tissue energy density and meat creatine content.

**Endurance and fitness** (`wave2-science-endurance-fitness.md`, rows S0661–S0731). The report gives the size, rate and asymmetry of aerobic training and detraining (the pooled VO₂max gains, Hickson's maintenance trials and ten-to-eleven-day half-time, bed-rest decline, the trainability spread), the muscle glycogen store and its depletion with hypoglycaemic fatigue and the carbohydrate, ketogenic and energy-availability effects, the iron effects on capacity and training adaptation, the dehydration evidence split between self-paced and clamped-intensity exercise, the caffeine, protein, vitamin D, B-vitamin, alcohol and sleep-loss effects, and body fat as carried load, as a non-factor in capacity per fat-free mass and as a heat-storage penalty. Most rows are meta-analytic and the rest small randomised or crossover trials, non-randomised training experiments, a family study and a handful of reviews and consensus statements. Every value is a central tendency for healthy adults aged 18 to 45, trained athletes or clinic patients measured in laboratories, well fed and on a schedule, and none was measured in people doing weeks of manual labour on scavenged food. Non-anaemic iron deficiency is the one place the pooled evidence disagrees with itself (positive in endurance athletes, null in general adults), and the two dehydration readings are kept as separate rows. The largest gaps are kinetic and interactive: no record gives the haemoglobin rise rate under oral iron, a per-hour cost of acute fasting on aerobic endurance, or the magnitude of the heat-and-dehydration interaction per degree of core temperature.

**Fatigue and sleep** (`wave2-science-fatigue-sleep.md`, rows S0732–S0862). The report supplies the two-process sleep model's fitted constants (wake and sleep time constants, thresholds, circadian amplitude), the dose-response of performance on sustained wakefulness and chronic restriction with its blood-alcohol equivalence, the few nutritional determinants of fatigue that survive the depletion-versus-supplementation distinction (iron deficiency without anaemia, mild dehydration, low energy availability), caffeine's pharmacokinetics, sleep cost, wake-promoting effect after sleep loss and withdrawal timeline, alcohol's two-halves-of-the-night sleep shape and next-day deficits, and exercise's mostly beneficial effect on the sleep that follows with its timing thresholds. Every value is a central tendency for healthy adults in laboratory protocols, pooled trials or wearable cohorts, except the ultra-endurance field studies, which are small and observational. The caffeine performance effects are conditioned on prior sleep loss, and the 5.0 h half-life rests on one trial's control arm rather than a pharmacokinetic review. Two publisher pages (the Borbély 2016 reappraisal and the EFSA caffeine opinion) could not be read at harvest, and their lines are open rows. The largest gaps are the absence of any measurement linking a nutritional or energy deficit to a rate of homeostatic fatigue accrual, of a caffeine tolerance decay rate, and of usable pooled estimates for alcohol's effect on total sleep time, efficiency and wake after sleep onset.

**Cognition, mood and perception** (`wave2-science-cognition-mood-perception.md`, rows S0863–S0945). The report gives vitamin A's selective loss of dark-adapted vision and its repletion time course (0.3 log units within a month of a large dose in eight patients, 0.71 log units after six weeks at 850 µg RAE/d, β-carotene no better than placebo in one trial and no worse than retinyl palmitate in another, iron and riboflavin co-limiting), zinc's thinner case as a second cause of night blindness, the sleep-loss, dehydration, hypoglycaemia, caffeine and alcohol effects on attention and reaction with the one published cross-calibration (24 h awake against 0.05 % blood alcohol), and the sleep-loss, dehydration, caffeine, exercise and micronutrient effects on affect, anxiety and stress. Much of the section is negative and is minted as such: acute fasting does nothing measurable to cognition, nutrient effects on hearing are under one decibel over years, and magnesium, B12 and vitamin D on anxiety, B vitamins on anxiety and glucose on mood carry no supported effect, so each null finding has a row of its own. Every value is a central tendency for a narrow population — the dark-adaptation repletion trials are almost all pregnant Nepali women, the zinc evidence six cirrhotics and one case, the hypoglycaemia magnitude type 2 diabetes patients, the iron-attention and iron-hearing evidence women and children — and none was measured in a survival population. Several meta-analyses report magnitudes the report would not carry at face value, and those caveats sit in the rows' ranges. The largest gap is interaction: no study measures two impairment drivers together, so combining sleep debt, dehydration, hypoglycaemia and alcohol rests on an unmeasured additivity.

**Healing, immunity and thermoregulation** (`wave2-science-healing-immunity-thermal.md`, rows S0946–S1050). The report gives the evidence for the mod's health effects: wound healing under protein-energy and micronutrient supplementation, bleeding under vitamin K restriction and scurvy, infection risk under malnutrition with the vitamin D, C, A, zinc and iron trials, fracture risk under calcium, vitamin D and protein, the hypothermia and heatstroke thresholds with the fat, energy, iron, alcohol and dehydration terms, and the upper levels and acute toxic doses of vitamins A, C, D and K, iron, zinc and selenium with the salt and water poisoning anchors. Its consistent finding is that restoring a deficient state helps and exceeding adequacy does not. The vitamin D respiratory effect is read from the 2025 update (OR 0.94, not significant) with the 2017 subgroup readings beside it, and dietary vitamin K restriction does not prolong clotting in healthy adults. Almost every effect size was measured in hospital inpatients, older adults, surgical patients, children in low-income countries or endurance athletes, so every row's population cell matters and transferring a magnitude to a healthy adult is a design decision; the one matching population is vitamin C and colds in exerting, cold-exposed adults. The toxicity thresholds are authority values (the NIH fact sheets restating the Food and Nutrition Board) filed under each nutrient's slug, with EFSA's lower selenium limit as a second row, and the largest gaps are a human effect size for vitamin A or dehydration on healing, any short-horizon nutritional effect on fracture risk, and the zinc emetic dose and the lethal salt dose, which stay open rather than guessed.

**The item pass** (Plan 6, rows S1181–S1220). Unlike the design-phase reports these rows were minted by task as the pipeline needed them, each citation resolved at its record. S1181–S1192 are the insect and invertebrate compositions the mapping's 18 literature records use: the per-order dry-matter table of Rumpold and Schlüter 2013 read from the secondary copies that reproduce it (the primary being paywalled, three copies agreeing cell for cell), the dry-matter fraction judgement, and the earthworm, woodlouse, centipede and millipede rows. S1193–S1208 are the phytate values: whole wheat, rice and maize through Lesjak 2019's copy of Schlemmer 2009, the gap row for the refined products, and the twelve PhyFoodComp medians (flours, breads, pasta, potato, Shredded Wheat, white, cooked and brown rice, rolled oats) read from the FAO/IZiNCG workbook. S1209–S1211 are the Atwater factors the record checks and the ethanol correction use (protein 4, fat 9, carbohydrate 4, fibre 2 and ethanol 7 kcal/g, FAO 2003). S1212 is Rickman 2007's qualitative finding on canning and freezing that the retention judgements lean on, S1213 the USDA retention table Release 6 the pipeline cites its codes from (24 of its rows defective and skipped), S1214 the iodine database Release 4.0, and S1215–S1220 the six legume and oilseed phytate families read from Schlemmer 2009 as Gupta 2015 reproduces it. Every value is a central tendency or a compiled range for the foods of its source, not for the game's items, which the mapping matches by a judgement its own notes state.

The eight reports and the plans that distilled them stay readable at the tag `design-phase-v1`.
