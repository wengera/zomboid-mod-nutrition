How to read it. Each `x34N.json` holds one session: `tool_diff` (the tool's own replay diff, HUNGER 0.002, es 0.005, `satiety.L` 0.5 %), `vs_replay` (live UNHAPPINESS and BOREDOM against the replay's, tolerance FLOOR_EPS plus 1e-3), `panic`, `rows_grade` (the row grades of that session), `events_by_kind`, `ring_first_age`, `ring_last_age`, `rows` and `driver_verdicts` (copied, do-not-cite). `rows.json` holds `row13` (the chain's grades) and `drinking_sodium`; `summary.json` the verdicts and the provenance (`regrade.fixed`). Inside `tool_diff.fields`, the ring-row copies under `row13.reconnect` and `drinking_sodium.by_day.<d>` the field names are literal keys with dots in them (`body.energyState`, `satiety.L`, `moodLast.u`, `_G.NutritionRevamp.server.writer.input.unhappyTarget`, `fluids.na_end`): a pointer names them as written. Chain day d runs from age 24d − 7 to 24d + 17. The design departures are x341's guide's; **the four-a-day BOREDOM resets bound every BOREDOM reading here** (`row13.rows.BORED_MAX.B_max`, `TRACK_B`'s excess, `MODEL_B`, `RISE_B_PER_H`, the day ends' BOREDOM and the release's B). **What is by construction (ruling 8-R)**: the twin is the mod's own kernel (`K.monotony.level` on the ring's record copy of the Mono450 entry, composed as the writer composes it), so TWIN tests the composition and the H1 read path (the entry as persisted, read by bracketed keys), not the level's formula; MODEL_U is `K.hybrid.floor` run on the writer's own input, the previous row's stat and the previous `moodLast`, so it tests that the floor's `Stats.set` landed each minute with no other writer of UNHAPPINESS; the oracle's day table is the same kernel minute by minute (`floor_run`), so the live-against-oracle agreement is arithmetic; the rise rates are the mod's constants (`moodRiseUnhappy` 22 and `moodRiseBored` 25 a game hour). What the live run adds is the path: the eat books only the count, the writer moves the stats a minute at a time, the record carries the entry and `moodLast` over two restarts and a reconnect, the client sees the stats, and vanilla adds nothing to UNHAPPINESS.
